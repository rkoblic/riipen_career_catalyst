# Project Arc Example: Mosaic Community Center

A worked example tracing one project, one team, and four individual students through all 15 weeks of the Career Catalyst experience. The purpose of this document is **internal validation**: to help us check that the course arc makes sense at the level of an actual team, that each student has enough meaningful work each week, and that the workload is balanced and well-supported across the term.

This is a planning artifact, not learner-facing content. It lives in `guides/` and should be updated as the course design evolves.

---

## The project

**Employer:** Mosaic Community Center, a 12-year-old nonprofit operating in an urban neighborhood of about 25,000 residents. Mosaic runs youth tutoring, job-readiness workshops, ESL classes, and a small food pantry. Eight staff, ~30 regular volunteers, ~$1.2M annual budget (mostly grants and individual donations).

**The brief:** Participation in Mosaic's programs has dropped roughly 30% over the past 18 months. The board doesn't know why and wants a clear-eyed assessment plus prioritized recommendations. Their working hypothesis is "we need better marketing." They can support modest pilot programs but don't have budget for major new initiatives.

**Final deliverable:** A community engagement strategy report (~25–30 pages) with:
- Executive summary
- Context (organization, community served, current programs and reach)
- Findings (what's actually driving the participation decline)
- Recommendations (what to do, prioritized)
- Implementation considerations
- Risks and unknowns
- Methodology and sources (appendix)

**Real answer the team will discover:** It isn't a marketing problem. Transportation and scheduling barriers prevent the community Mosaic most needs to serve from getting to programs. The recommendation will become a small satellite-location pilot at a partner community center 2 km away.

---

## The team

Four students, each with a distinct background and a clear section to own. Section ownership is decided in Week 6, but the team's strengths shape what gets assigned to whom.

**Jordan** — Fourth-year sociology student. Comfortable with interviews, qualitative coding, community-based research. Volunteered at a similar nonprofit last year. **Owns: Findings section** (the qualitative case for the access hypothesis, anchored in community member voices).

**Aisha** — Third-year business student, marketing concentration. Strong writer, comfortable with data, no nonprofit experience but eager. **Owns: Context section + Executive Summary** (the framing and the polished synthesis).

**Marcus** — Fourth-year urban planning student. Has researched transportation and access barriers in prior coursework. **Owns: Implementation Considerations** (the access analysis, the satellite-location pilot proposal, the practical "how").

**Priya** — Third-year communications student with project management instincts. **Owns: Risks and Unknowns + Methodology Appendix + integration work** (formatting, terminology consistency, final assembly, Change Log).

## Week 1 — Course orientation

**Team:** No teams yet. Course orientation, Student Intake Questionnaire, project preference submission.

- **Jordan:** Submits intake. Lists community-based research projects high in preferences. Drafts a short rationale.
- **Aisha:** Submits intake. Lists strategy and marketing-adjacent projects high. Drafts rationale.
- **Marcus:** Submits intake. Lists urban and community-engagement projects high. Drafts rationale.
- **Priya:** Submits intake. Lists communications and project management work high. Drafts rationale.

---

## Week 2 — Team formation, employer outreach

**Team:** Placed on Mosaic project. First team meeting (~90 min). Drafts Team Charter. Initial reconnaissance research on Mosaic. Outreach email to the executive director.

- **Jordan:** First team meeting. Volunteers to lead community-facing research if relevant. Reconnaissance: Mosaic's website, Charity Navigator, recent local news mentions. Drafts the team values portion of the charter.
- **Aisha:** First team meeting. Volunteers to draft the outreach email to the ED. Reconnaissance focused on Mosaic's stated programs and audience profile. Sends the outreach email.
- **Marcus:** First team meeting. Flags interest in the access angle. Reconnaissance on the neighborhood Mosaic serves: demographics, transit infrastructure, comparable centers in the city.
- **Priya:** First team meeting. Volunteers as the team's coordination owner. Sets up the shared workspace. Drafts meeting cadence and accountability sections of the charter. Reconnaissance focused on Mosaic's history and recent organizational changes.

---

## Week 3 — Kickoff meeting, post-kickoff research, Reflection #1

**Team:** Pre-kickoff prep meeting. Kickoff with the executive director (60 min). Post-kickoff debrief and Kickoff Summary. Targeted research now that scope is confirmed. Reflection #1 (graded).

- **Jordan:** Active questioner at the kickoff. Captures what the ED says about community member engagement specifically. Post-kickoff research on community engagement frameworks. Reflection #1 anchored on a moment of surprise during the kickoff.
- **Aisha:** Lead presenter at the kickoff (introduces team, walks the agenda, manages time). Drafts the Kickoff Summary based on team notes. Post-kickoff research on Mosaic's recent public-facing materials. Reflection #1 on what it felt like to lead a professional client meeting for the first time.
- **Marcus:** Asks targeted kickoff questions about geographic reach. Notes ED's offhand observation that participation has been uneven across neighborhoods. Post-kickoff research on transit access mapping and multi-site nonprofit models. Reflection #1.
- **Priya:** Note-taker at the kickoff (focuses on exact language and direct quotes). Drafts the post-kickoff follow-up email. Coordinates the debrief. Post-kickoff research on Mosaic's annual reports and grant filings. Reflection #1.

---

## Week 4 — Context Analysis & Project Plan (graded, 10%)

**Team:** First major graded team deliverable. Team synthesizes research into a coherent context analysis and proposes a project plan. Submitted to the employer ahead of the Direction Check next week.

- **Jordan:** Drafts the section on the community Mosaic serves (demographics, needs, what's known about engagement). Brings the early hypothesis that participation barriers may not match Mosaic's "marketing problem" framing.
- **Aisha:** Drafts the framing intro and the section on Mosaic's existing programs and reach. Owns the project plan section (timeline, milestones, deliverable structure). Coordinates voice and tone across the document.
- **Marcus:** Drafts the section on geographic and access landscape. Contributes the early analytical case that access (not awareness) is the more likely driver of decline.
- **Priya:** Drafts the methodology section and the risks section of the project plan. Coordinates integration of all sections. Catches inconsistencies and formats the final document for submission.

---

## Week 5 — Direction Check, Direction Check Summary, plan revisions

**Team:** Direction Check meeting (60 min) with the ED and one board member. The team's hypothesis (access, not awareness) is largely confirmed, but the ED pushes back on a multi-site satellite recommendation as too operationally complex without more evidence. Team revises plan to add a community member listening exercise.

- **Jordan:** Presents the team's preliminary findings and the access hypothesis. Captures the ED's pushback in the post-meeting debrief. Identifies that the team needs primary qualitative data from community members to make the case strong enough.
- **Aisha:** Opens and frames the Direction Check meeting. Drafts the Direction Check Summary. Coordinates the team's revision of the project plan to incorporate the ED's feedback.
- **Marcus:** Presents the access analysis and receives the operational pushback on the multi-site idea. Adjusts approach: instead of a major recommendation, will frame Implementation as a low-cost pilot worth testing.
- **Priya:** Note-taker at the Direction Check (captures exact language and concerns). Drafts the formal Direction Check Summary template. Updates the project plan and methodology to reflect the new community-listening exercise. Coordinates section ownership for the production phase.

---

## Week 6 — Reflection #2, production startup, individual research, drafting begins

**Team:** Reflection #2 closes Discovery. Team meets to plan production: section ownership confirmed, document setup, formatting, terminology. Each student does section-specific research and starts drafting their assigned piece.

- **Jordan:** Submits Reflection #2 (anchored on the Direction Check pushback moment and what it revealed about handling disagreement). Section-specific research: re-reads kickoff notes for direct quotes, conducts 4–6 short community member conversations, reviews published research on participation barriers in comparable programs. Outlines findings by theme. Begins drafting.
- **Aisha:** Submits Reflection #2 (anchored on what it felt like to lead at the Direction Check). Section-specific research: Mosaic's recent grant materials, annual reports, board minutes for context-level material. Outlines context section. Begins drafting context (will write Executive Summary last). Sets up the team's formatting standards.
- **Marcus:** Submits Reflection #2 (anchored on the Context Analysis production process — what working under deadline pressure with a team revealed). Section-specific research: pulls together transit data, looks up 2–3 comparable satellite-program case studies, sketches the access analysis. Begins drafting an implementation section that frames the pilot as a low-cost test.
- **Priya:** Submits Reflection #2 (anchored on integrating the Context Analysis — what coordinating four people's work revealed about her own role). Section-specific research: reviews comparable risks and methodology sections from other reports for structural ideas. Sets up the shared workspace with formatting. Drafts risks section first. Begins methodology appendix.

---

## Week 7 — Check-In #1, continued production

**Team:** First Check-In with the employer (60 min, questions-based working session). Team brings real production questions. Continued individual drafting. WIP Summary submitted.

- **Jordan:** Findings section ~60% drafted. Brings to Check-In #1: "We have qualitative data from six community members about transportation barriers. Does this match what you're seeing on the ground at the center?" Continues drafting after the meeting, incorporating the ED's affirmation.
- **Aisha:** Context section largely drafted. Brings to Check-In #1: "We're framing the participation decline as an access issue, not a marketing one. Is there anything about Mosaic's history with marketing efforts we should acknowledge?" Continues drafting after the meeting. Begins thinking about Executive Summary structure.
- **Marcus:** Implementation section is the most uncertain piece. Brings to Check-In #1: "We're recommending a pilot at one location, not multiple. Does the board have any specific locations they've considered?" Gets useful guidance: a partner community center 2 km away might host. Substantially updates implementation section.
- **Priya:** Plays a coordination role at Check-In #1. Asks: "What format would be most useful for the Change Log when we share the final package?" Continues integrating sections as they come in. Drafts methodology appendix in parallel. Reviews Marcus's implementation draft for clarity.

---

## Week 8 — Process feedback, Peer Evaluation #1 (graded)

**Team:** Process Check-In #1 feedback. Each student writes Peer Evaluation #1 (graded individual, ~10% via Professional Engagement). Continued production with revisions based on feedback.

- **Jordan:** Revises findings to incorporate ED's feedback (adds a paragraph on scheduling barriers the ED mentioned). Drafts Peer Eval #1 with specific behavioral observations of each teammate. Self-evaluation honest about being slow on integration tasks.
- **Aisha:** Major revision of context to acknowledge Mosaic's previous marketing efforts. Drafts Peer Eval #1: notes Marcus's adaptability after the satellite pushback. Self-evaluation honest about over-leading meetings sometimes.
- **Marcus:** Substantially rewrites implementation now that the partner community center is on the table. Reaches out to that center informally for a quick conversation. Drafts Peer Eval #1: notes Jordan's strong qualitative work. Self-evaluation honest about under-communicating during research.
- **Priya:** Drafts Peer Eval #1: notes Aisha's lead role and Marcus's adaptability. Self-evaluation honest about being too quiet in early meetings. Continues integration work, with more drafted material to integrate. Coordinates the team's first standing Status Update to the employer.

---

## Week 9 — Draft deliverable, Reflection #3, Check-In #2

**Team:** Submit Draft Deliverable as a checkpoint (full assembled draft). Check-In #2 with employer (questions-based, focused on production challenges and scope decisions). Reflection #3 due (graded). Internal review gate before submission.

- **Jordan:** Findings substantially drafted with revisions. Contributes to internal team review. Reflection #3 anchored on the moment one community member's interview shifted Jordan's understanding of the access issue.
- **Aisha:** Context substantially drafted. Begins Executive Summary now that the rest of the document is taking shape. Contributes to internal review. Reflection #3 anchored on receiving Peer Eval #1 feedback.
- **Marcus:** Implementation substantially drafted with the partner-location pilot incorporated. Brings to Check-In #2: "We're recommending a 6-month pilot at the partner location with these specific success metrics. Does this feel feasible?" Reflection #3 anchored on the moment in Check-In #1 when the ED gave him the partner-location lead.
- **Priya:** In heavy integration mode. Draft Deliverable assembled and submitted with the team. Reviews each section for consistency, terminology, formatting. Risks section updated for the partner-location-based pilot. Reflection #3 anchored on what writing detailed Peer Eval feedback revealed about her own assumptions.

---

## Week 10 — Connect theory and practice, Change Log introduced

**Team:** Step back from production. Process Check-In #2 feedback. Continue integration. Change Log introduced and team begins formal iteration tracking.

- **Jordan:** Refines findings based on Check-In #2 feedback. Adds more direct community member quotes to strengthen the evidence base. Begins making first Change Log entries.
- **Aisha:** Continues drafting Executive Summary. Refines context. Tries the AI-as-quality-reviewer prompt on her sections. Updates Change Log with her contributions.
- **Marcus:** Implementation substantially complete. Refines the success metrics for the pilot recommendation. Updates Change Log with the pilot scope history (multi-site → single-site → partner-location).
- **Priya:** Major integration pass. Begins formal Change Log on behalf of the team. Identifies remaining gaps in the assembled draft. Prepares the package for Near-Final Review next week.

---

## Week 11 — Near-Final Package, employer Near-Final Review

**Team:** Polish, refinement, integration. Submit Near-Final Package. Employer Near-Final Review meeting (60 min, document-based).

- **Jordan:** Final round of edits to findings. Adds executive-level summary boxes per the team's formatting standards. Reviews other sections for coherence with findings.
- **Aisha:** Executive Summary substantially drafted. Final polish on context and exec summary. Coordinates voice and tone across the document. Lead presenter at the Near-Final Review.
- **Marcus:** Implementation finalized. Adds visuals (transit access map, pilot timeline). Brings to Near-Final Review: "Does the proposed timeline feel realistic given the partner center's capacity?"
- **Priya:** Major integration push. Final formatting pass. Assembles the methodology appendix. Updates Change Log with all the iterations from Weeks 6–11. Submits Near-Final Package on behalf of the team.

---

## Week 12 — Finalize deliverables, Reflection #4 (graded), plan presentation

**Team:** Finalize deliverable based on Near-Final Review feedback. Plan Final Presentation for Week 15. Reflection #4 due (graded).

- **Jordan:** Final refinements to findings. Begins thinking about presentation role. Reflection #4 anchored on the gap between what Jordan thought the project would be in Week 4 and what it actually became.
- **Aisha:** Final pass on Executive Summary. Drafts the Presentation Outline (lead writer for the deck structure). Reflection #4 on what the marketing-focused initial framing missed.
- **Marcus:** Implementation finalized with one small addition based on Near-Final Review. Contributes to Presentation Outline (will present the access and implementation slides). Reflection #4 on how the satellite-location pivot changed his understanding of professional pushback.
- **Priya:** Final integration pass. Updates Change Log to its final form. Reflection #4 on the role of integration work and what coordinating four voices revealed about her own professional strengths.

---

## Week 13 — Final Deliverable submission (graded, 30%)

**Team:** Final assembly. Submit Final Deliverables (graded team submission, 30% of course grade). Optional Final Check-In with employer cancelled (no remaining substantive questions).

- **Jordan:** Final read-through. Sign-off on findings.
- **Aisha:** Final read-through. Sign-off on context and Executive Summary.
- **Marcus:** Final read-through. Sign-off on implementation.
- **Priya:** Final read-through and assembly. Submits Final Deliverables on behalf of the team. Includes Change Log and AI disclosure.

---

## Week 14 — Build presentation, begin Reflection #5 and Peer Evaluation #2

**Team:** Build the Final Presentation deck. Rehearse. Begin Reflection #5 and Peer Evaluation #2 (both finalized in Week 15).

- **Jordan:** Builds findings slides. Will present the findings section live. Begins Reflection #5 (anchored on the project as a whole).
- **Aisha:** Builds context and recommendation slides. Will open and close the presentation. Begins Reflection #5.
- **Marcus:** Builds implementation slides. Will present the recommendation and implementation portion. Begins Reflection #5 and Peer Eval #2.
- **Priya:** Coordinates deck integration. Builds methodology and risks slides (or folds them into other slides). Will handle Q&A coordination during the presentation. Begins Reflection #5 and Peer Eval #2.

---

## Week 15 — Final Presentation, Reflection #5, Peer Evaluation #2

**Team:** Final Presentation with the employer (60 min). Post-presentation debrief and team closing. Submit Reflection #5 and Peer Evaluation #2. Course closes.

- **Jordan:** Presents the findings section. Reflection #5 anchored on how Jordan's understanding of community-based research evolved across the term. Submits Peer Eval #2.
- **Aisha:** Opens and closes the Final Presentation. Reflection #5 anchored on the experience of leading a professional client engagement from Week 3 onward. Submits Peer Eval #2.
- **Marcus:** Presents the recommendation and implementation. Reflection #5 on how the satellite-location pivot reshaped his understanding of professional adaptability. Submits Peer Eval #2.
- **Priya:** Coordinates the post-presentation debrief and team closing. Reflection #5 on the role of integration work as a distinct professional contribution. Submits Peer Eval #2.

## Notes and observations

This section captures what we notice while reading the arc above. Each note should help us decide whether to adjust course design, content, or pacing.

### Workload balance

- The back third of the course (Weeks 11–15) is consistently heavy with no breathing room: five consecutive ~8-hour weeks, four with a graded individual or team submission attached. Burnout in the final stretch shows up at exactly the moment the work is most visible to the employer. Worth investigating whether some of the Week 14/15 reflection and peer eval prep can be pulled forward, or whether one of Weeks 12 or 13 can be lightened.
- Week 6 is densely packed: Reflection #2 + planning meeting + individual research + drafting begins. May be over-budgeted relative to the design doc's 560 minutes, especially for Jordan (see "where individuals have too much" below).
- Weeks 1 and 13 (sign-off) are light by design, which is appropriate.

### Where the arc holds together well

- The narrative pivot (multi-site → single-site → partner-location pilot) is realistic and gives the team something specific to learn from. Real consulting work iterates like this.
- Each student has a defensible reason to own their section based on actual background and strength.
- Aisha's leadership arc has continuity across kickoff, Direction Check, Near-Final Review, and Final Presentation, giving her something concrete to reflect on across multiple reflections. Same for Marcus with the satellite-pivot story.
- The Direction Check pushback in Week 5 does real work on the recommendation's shape, exactly the value the Direction Check is supposed to add.
- Reflection anchors generally track to events built into earlier weeks, so reflection prompts have something to hook into.

### Where the arc feels thin or under-supported

- **Jordan's Week 6 community interviews are logistically aggressive.** Identifying community members willing to talk to a student team takes coordination, requires the ED's facilitation, and may need ethics review at some institutions. Conducting 4–6 conversations in one week alongside drafting and Reflection #2 is unrealistic for a part-time student commitment. Realistic alternatives: the ED introduces 1–2 community members; a single small focus group rather than individual interviews; or reliance on secondary qualitative sources Mosaic has already gathered (program survey responses, intake forms, prior board reports).
- **The partner community center contact in Week 7 is convenient.** Marcus reaching out to another nonprofit and getting a productive conversation in a single week works in the arc, but it's a bit of a deus ex machina. In real life this could take longer or not happen at all.
- **The recommendation evolves a lot.** Multi-site → single-site → partner-location → 6-month pilot with metrics. Each change is defensible on its own, but in aggregate the team's central recommendation reshapes four times. Could undermine team confidence in their own work. Might be worth one of the reflections (probably Reflection #4) explicitly addressing the gap between Week 4 plan and Week 13 reality.
- **Qualitative analysis is under-scaffolded.** The arc relies on Jordan doing meaningful qualitative work — but the curriculum may not actually walk students through how to extract themes from interview transcripts in a defensible way. See the Week 6 coverage discussion.

### Where any individual team member has too little to do

- **Marcus's early production weeks (Week 6, first half) may be light.** His substantial content emerges only after Check-In #1 in Week 7 when the partner-center lead surfaces. Before that, he's doing access analysis and outlining without much to commit to.
- **Priya's Weeks 2–3 may feel coordinator-y without much output.** Setting up shared workspaces and drafting charter sections is real work, but it's vague compared to teammates with clearer deliverables. Worth thinking about whether the curriculum gives integration-role students a clearer "this is your job this week" narrative early on.

### Where any individual has too much

- **Jordan in Week 6** (if interviews stay as written): Reflection #2 + community interviews + outlining findings + drafting + reading existing materials is too much for one ~9-hour week. This is the most overloaded student-week in the arc.
- **Priya in Weeks 11–13.** She owns assembly across the heaviest production weeks. The integration role compounds at exactly the moment the team is most stressed. Worth checking whether other team members can absorb integration tasks during the crunch, or whether the integration owner role needs more explicit support during the back third.

### Open questions for course design

- **Employer touchpoint cadence.** 6–7 meetings over 12 active weeks (kickoff, Direction Check, Check-In #1, Check-In #2, Near-Final Review, optional Final Check-In, Final Presentation) plus async status updates may be too much for a small nonprofit ED. Worth checking what real Riipen partners typically agree to. The "optional" Final Check-In in W13 may end up cancelled more often than not, and the Near-Final Review may get compressed or made async in practice.
- **Reflection anchor diversity for the integration role.** Priya's five reflections all on integration work risks repetition. Each reflection needs a distinct anchor, not five variations of "I coordinated things and it was hard." Worth thinking about whether the reflection prompts give integration-role students enough distinct experiences to draw on.
- **The arc is idealized.** No missed meetings, no midterms collisions, no illness, no team conflict beyond what the curriculum can absorb. Worth eventually running a "stress test" version where one student disappears for a week mid-production, or where the team has a real disagreement they can't resolve internally, to see whether the curriculum supports the team through it.
- **Qualitative research scaffolding.** If we expect students to do meaningful qualitative work (interviews, focus groups, ethnographic observation), do we scaffold it anywhere? Saying "interviews are an option" in a research module isn't the same as teaching how to plan, conduct, and synthesize qualitative material. May need a dedicated resource or curated link.
