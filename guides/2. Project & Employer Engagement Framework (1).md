# Project & Employer Engagement Framework

*A standardized, scalable model for employer-connected project learning*

# **Overview**

This document defines the **Project & Employer Engagement Framework** that underpins the Riipen Experience course. The framework establishes a **consistent, repeatable model** for how employer-sponsored projects are integrated into learning experiences, how employers participate, and how feedback and accountability are structured.

While instantiated here within a 15-week, for-credit course, the framework itself is **modular and reusable**. It is designed to support employer-connected learning across a range of contexts—including credit-bearing courses, co-curricular experiences, and scaffolded learning pathways—without requiring bespoke design or ongoing customization for each institution or employer.

# **Design Intent**

The framework is intentionally designed to balance four priorities:

1. **Authenticity:** Students work on real projects with real external stakeholders

2. **Scalability:** Employer engagement remains consistent and low-burden at scale

3. **Academic integrity:** Faculty retain responsibility for learning design and assessment

4. **Comparability:** Diverse projects can be evaluated within a common structure

To achieve this, the framework clearly defines **what is fixed** and **what is flexible**.

# **Employer Role Definition**

Employers participate as **project sponsors and authentic stakeholders**, not as instructors or evaluators of academic performance.

### **Employer responsibilities**

* Provide a clearly scoped, real-world project   
* Designate a primary point of contact  
* Participate in seven structured touchpoints across the learning experience  
* Offer directional and qualitative feedback on student work  
* Model professional expectations and workplace context

### **Employer non-responsibilities**

* No grading or academic assessment  
* No curriculum or assignment design  
* No student supervision or management  
* No ad hoc meetings outside defined touchpoints

This role clarity ensures employer engagement is **meaningful, bounded, and repeatable**, enabling consistent participation across institutions and terms.

# **Project Integration Rules**

All employer projects are embedded within a **fixed learning structure** that carries the pedagogy, assessment, and accountability—independent of project content.

### **Fixed elements**

* Defined project lifecycle aligned to the learning arc  
* Standardized deliverables and checkpoints  
* Consistent feedback and reflection moments  
* Common competency and assessment framework  
* Structured employer touchpoint cadence

### **Variable elements**

* Project topic, domain, and industry  
* Project category (selected from a standardized set)  
* Analytical or applied focus  
* Final deliverable format (e.g., analysis, strategy, recommendations, prototype)

This separation allows projects to vary meaningfully while remaining **comparable, assessable, and operationally manageable** within a single course or across multiple learning contexts.

# **Standardized Project Categories**

To support scalability, comparability, and ease of employer participation, Riipen Experience projects are organized into a small set of standardized project categories. These categories represent common types of work that appear across industries and organizational contexts and can be executed within the same learning structure and assessment framework.

Employers select (or are guided to) one of these categories during project scoping. The specific topic, organization, and context vary, but the analytical approach, deliverable expectations, and feedback cadence remain consistent.

1. **Market, User & Context Analysis**  
   *Understanding needs, audiences, and environments*  
   Incudes:  
   * Market research  
   * Customer / user discovery  
   * Audience segmentation & personas  
   * User journey or experience mapping  
   * Needs assessments (customer, community, stakeholder)

   

2. **Competitive & Landscape Intelligence**  
   *Understanding positioning and external forces*  
   Includes:  
   * Competitive analysis  
   * Industry or ecosystem landscape analysis  
   * Benchmarking & best-practice scans  
   * Trend analysis & implications  
   * White-space or opportunity mapping

   

3. **Strategy & Growth Planning**  
   *Turning insight into direction and prioritization*  
   Includes:  
   * Strategic planning (initiative- or function-level)  
   * Growth or expansion strategy  
   * Product / service positioning strategy  
   * Program or initiative roadmapping  
   * Market entry or scaling analysis

   

4. **Marketing, Brand & Engagement Strategy**  
   Improving how organizations reach and engage audiences  
   Includes:  
   * Digital or social media audits  
   * Content and communications strategy  
   * Brand or messaging audits  
   * Campaign concept development  
   * Customer, community, or stakeholder engagement strategies

   

5. **Operations, Program & Experience Improvement**  
   *Making existing systems work better*  
   Includes:  
   * Process or workflow analysis  
   * Service or program effectiveness reviews  
   * Product, service, or program optimization  
   * Internal communication or coordination reviews  
   * Impact, outcomes, or equity-focused assessments

### **Why Standardized Project Categories Matter**

Standardized project categories reduce employer onboarding friction, prevent ad hoc project design, and ensure that student work remains comparable across teams and terms. Employers gain clarity on what to expect; faculty gain consistency in supervision and assessment; students gain exposure to transferable project types that appear across professional contexts.

# **Employer Touchpoint Cadence**

Employer engagement is structured around **seven predefined touchpoints**, aligned to natural project milestones. Each touchpoint has a clear purpose, agenda, and time expectation.

| Touchpoint | Timing | Duration | Purpose |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Kickoff Meeting** | Week 3 | 60 min | Establish relationship, clarify scope and success criteria, answer student questions |
| **Direction Check** | Week 5 | 30 min | Review proposed approach before students commit; catch misalignment early |
| **Progress Review \#1** | Week 7 | 30 min | Substantive feedback on early draft work; course correction while time remains |
| **Progress Review \#2** | Week 9 | 30 min | Continued feedback on work-in-progress; address any emerging issues |
| **Progress Review \#3** | Week 11 | 30 min | Near-final review; feedback on assembled deliverable package |
| **Progress Review \#4 (Final Review)** | Week 13 | 30 min | Final review of completed deliverables before formal presentation |
| **Final Presentation** | Week 15 | 30 min | Formal presentation of completed work; closure; final feedback |

**Typical employer commitment:** 30–60 minutes per touchpoint; approximately 4-5 total hours across a full term, in addition to an additional 2-3 hours of asynchronous feedback or communication.

Agendas, preparation guidance, and feedback prompts are standardized and provided in advance.

# **Communication Boundaries & Flow**

The framework is designed to minimize coordination overhead while maintaining professional authenticity.

* All employer interaction is **scheduled, structured, and time-bound**  
* Students are responsible for preparing materials and documenting outcomes  
* Informal or ad hoc communication is intentionally avoided  
* Platform-based summaries and surveys capture feedback without additional meetings

This approach reduces friction for employers while reinforcing student accountability and professionalism.

# **Feedback & Iteration Model**

Employer input is one component of a **multi-source feedback system** that supports learning and iteration without over-relying on any single stakeholder.

### **Employer feedback focuses on:**

* Project direction and alignment  
* Practical relevance and feasibility  
* Clarity, professionalism, and responsiveness  
* Fit between deliverables and real-world use

### **How feedback is captured:**

* Live discussion during touchpoints  
* Brief structured surveys at defined moments  
* Student-generated summaries documenting how feedback was interpreted and applied

Employer feedback contributes to learning and professional engagement evidence, while **faculty retain responsibility for academic evaluation**.

# **Quality Assurance & Accountability**

The framework separates **authentic external validation** from **academic assessment**.

* Employers provide real-world perspective and directional input  
* Faculty assess learning using consistent rubrics and evidence  
* Multiple evidence sources are triangulated (deliverables, reflections, peer input, employer feedback)  
* No single input determines outcomes

This ensures rigor, fairness, and consistency while preserving the value of employer engagement. Because all projects align to a common set of project categories and follow the same lifecycle, deliverables can be assessed consistently even when project content varies significantly.

# **Framework Reuse Across Learning Contexts**

Because employer participation, project integration, and feedback structures are defined **independently of term length, credit structure, or discipline**, this framework can be reused across contexts, including:

* For-credit interdisciplinary courses  
* Short-form or modular experiential offerings  
* Co-curricular or noncredit programs  
* Scaffolded pathways where multiple projects build over time  
* Pilot implementations prior to curricular adoption

In all cases, the **employer role definition, touchpoint cadence, and feedback architecture remain fixed**, while timing and deliverables can be adapted to fit the learning environment.
