## Part 1: Organizational & project context

*Purpose: Demonstrate that your team understands the organization and project well enough to analyze them. This is where the employer decides whether to trust the rest of the document. Target length is roughly 1 page.*

*This should not just restate the brief. Show that you were listening at the kickoff, that you've done your homework, and that you understand the problem. If the kickoff revealed nuances the brief didn't capture, this is where you surface them. Cover the organization, the project as you understand it, and any context that shapes how you're approaching the work.*




---

## Part 2: Research findings

*Purpose: Show what your team learned through independent research and what it means for the project. Synthesis is what makes this section valuable, not the facts themselves. Target length is roughly 1.5–2 pages.*

*Your employer knows their organization from the inside. What they may not have is an outside perspective. Bring something new: patterns you noticed, gaps you identified, context the employer may not have considered. Organize findings around themes, not around who did which piece of research. Cite sources for any factual claim.*

## Part 3: Strategic implications

*Purpose: Connect your research to a point of view on the project. Move from describing what you found to saying what should happen as a result. This is the section your employer cares about most. Target length is roughly 1 page.*

*A strong strategic implications section gives the employer confidence that your team can turn findings into recommendations. Cover: what the research means, the direction your team is taking and why, the tradeoffs that direction implies, and the assumptions your recommendation rests on.*




### Assumptions we're making

*The premises your strategic direction depends on. Name them so the employer can confirm or correct them at the Direction Check. An unnamed assumption that turns out to be wrong is much harder to recover from than a named one.*

## Part 4: Project plan

*Purpose: Tell the employer what to expect: what you'll deliver, when, what you consider out of scope, and what might go wrong. A clear plan with named tradeoffs and identified risks signals that your team is being realistic and gives the employer a chance to course-correct before you start building. Target length is roughly 1.5–2 pages.*

### What's in scope

*Specific work your team will produce. Be concrete: name the artifacts, the level of polish, the audience they're for.*




### What's out of scope

*Things the team is deliberately not doing, even if they're related to the project. Naming these protects against scope creep and gives the employer a chance to push back if they expected something that isn't on your list.*




### Approach

*How your team plans to do the work. Methods, frameworks, sequence of phases — whatever the project calls for. This is the team's working approach, not a restatement of the course structure.*
