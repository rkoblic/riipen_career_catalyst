# End-of-Project Self-Evaluation — Form build content

For the Riipen Experience Manager building this survey in the survey tool. Contains the headers, learner-facing text, scales, and field types.

This is a standalone survey, parallel to the [End-of-Project Peer Evaluation](end-of-project-peer-evaluation-form-build.md). The learner completes both the same week. The self-evaluation uses the same four BARS dimensions and the same anchors as the peer evaluation — what changes is that the learner is rating themselves, calibrated across the full fifteen-week project arc.

## Submission settings

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Submission type | Individual |
| Grading | Ungraded. Used by the instructor as a calibration signal against the peer view. Not its own gradebook item inside Professional Engagement. |
| Timing | Week 15 (same week as the End-of-Project Peer Evaluation). Work may begin in Week 14. |
| Editable after submission? | No |
| Visibility | Instructor only. Self-ratings are not shared with teammates and do not appear in the anonymized peer feedback report. |

## Pre-evaluation framing

Shown above Part A on the form.

This is your end-of-project self-evaluation, the companion to the End-of-Project Peer Evaluation you are also completing this week. You rate yourself on the same four dimensions your teammates rate you on, calibrated across the full fifteen-week project arc. The self-evaluation is not graded and does not affect your peer evaluation score. Your instructor uses it as a calibration signal: large gaps between how you see your own contribution across the project and how your teammates see it are worth understanding.

Rate yourself honestly. The ratings hardest to give yourself honestly are usually the most informative. Behavioral evidence is encouraged but not required. Calibrate your ratings to behavior across the whole project, not the last thing you remember.

## Part A — Self-rating on the four BARS dimensions

Each dimension uses a 1–4 Behaviorally Anchored Rating Scale (radio buttons), followed by an optional short-text evidence field.

Section heading shown to learner: **Rate yourself on the four dimensions**

### A1. Reliability

Sub-heading question shown to learner: *Across the project, did I do what I said I would do?*

Field type: Radio buttons (single select), 1–4
Required: Yes

Options shown to learner:

| Rating | Descriptor | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | Consistently dependable | Delivered on every commitment on time or early. Showed up to meetings prepared. Notified the team proactively if a timeline was at risk. Teammates trusted this person to follow through without reminders. |
| 3 | Generally reliable | Met most deadlines and showed up prepared for meetings. Occasionally needed a reminder but followed through when prompted. Did not cause the team to miss a milestone. |
| 2 | Inconsistently reliable | Missed a deadline or came to a meeting unprepared more than once. Required teammates to follow up or compensate. Caused at least one delay or rework cycle for the team. |
| 1 | Unreliable | Regularly missed commitments or failed to follow through. Team could not count on this person to deliver what they agreed to. Other members had to absorb their responsibilities. |

Evidence field type: Long text (textarea)
Evidence field required: No

Evidence prompt shown to learner:

Optional. Briefly describe a specific commitment you made across the project and how you followed through (or didn't).

### A2. Contribution

Sub-heading question shown to learner: *Across the project, did I do my fair share and make the work better?*

Field type: Radio buttons (single select), 1–4
Required: Yes

Options shown to learner:

| Rating | Descriptor | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | Drives the work forward | Completed a fair share and actively looked for ways to contribute more. Volunteered for tasks when the team needed it. Work quality was strong enough that teammates rarely needed to revise it. Brought ideas that improved the project. |
| 3 | Pulls their weight | Completed assigned work to an acceptable standard. Did what was asked without needing excessive direction. Work may have needed some editing but did not require fundamental rework. |
| 2 | Under-contributing | Needed reminders to complete assigned work or produced work that required significant revision by teammates. Did not volunteer for additional tasks even when the team was stretched. |
| 1 | Not contributing | Did not complete assigned work or relied on others to cover responsibilities. Work product, if submitted, was unusable without being essentially redone by a teammate. |

Evidence field type: Long text (textarea)
Evidence field required: No

Evidence prompt shown to learner:

Optional. Briefly describe a specific piece of work you produced or a specific way you contributed (or did not) across the project.

### A3. Constructive Engagement

Sub-heading question shown to learner: *Across the project, did I participate in a way that made the team's work better?*

Field type: Radio buttons (single select), 1–4
Required: Yes

Options shown to learner:

| Rating | Descriptor | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | Actively improves team communication | Regularly offered specific, actionable input that made the team's work better. Engaged thoughtfully with others' ideas, building on them, asking questions, and identifying gaps. Navigated disagreements by focusing on the work rather than the person. |
| 3 | Participates constructively | Participated in team discussions and responded to feedback from others. Shared perspective when asked. Did not create communication friction. Accepted feedback without defensiveness. |
| 2 | Minimally engaged | Participated only when directly prompted or gave feedback that was vague and unhelpful (e.g., "looks good" without specifics). Did not initiate discussion or build on others' ideas. Sometimes agreed with everything to avoid conflict. |
| 1 | Disengaged or counterproductive | Did not engage meaningfully in team discussions. Dominated without listening, dismissed others' contributions, or created an environment where teammates hesitated to share ideas. |

Evidence field type: Long text (textarea)
Evidence field required: No

Evidence prompt shown to learner:

Optional. Briefly describe a specific instance where your communication or feedback helped (or hindered) the team's work.

### A4. Accountability

Sub-heading question shown to learner: *Across the project, did I take responsibility for team outcomes, not just my own section?*

Field type: Radio buttons (single select), 1–4
Required: Yes

Options shown to learner:

| Rating | Descriptor | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | Owns team outcomes | Took responsibility for the team's success, not just an individual section. Identified problems proactively and mobilized the team to address them. When something went wrong, focused on solutions rather than blame. Supported teammates who were struggling. |
| 3 | Takes personal responsibility | Took responsibility for own work and supported the team's goals. Acknowledged when a mistake had been made. Participated in problem-solving when issues arose. |
| 2 | Deflects or waits | Tended to deflect responsibility when things went wrong or waited for others to address problems. Blamed external factors (the employer, the timeline, the tools) rather than examining own role. |
| 1 | Avoids responsibility | Did not take responsibility for outcomes. Blamed teammates or external factors. Did not participate in addressing team challenges. Actively undermined team cohesion through gossip, complaints, or disengagement. |

Evidence field type: Long text (textarea)
Evidence field required: No

Evidence prompt shown to learner:

Optional. Briefly describe a specific situation where you took responsibility for a team outcome (or failed to do so).
