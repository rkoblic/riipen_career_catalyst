# Mid-Project Self-Evaluation — Form build content

For the Riipen Experience Manager building this survey in the survey tool. Contains the headers, learner-facing text, scales, and field types.

This is a standalone survey, parallel to the [Mid-Project Peer Evaluation](mid-project-peer-evaluation-form-build.md). The learner completes both the same week. The self-evaluation uses the same four BARS dimensions and the same anchors as the peer evaluation — what changes is that the learner is rating themselves.

## Submission settings

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Submission type | Individual |
| Grading | Ungraded. Used by the instructor as a calibration signal against the peer view. Not its own gradebook item inside Professional Engagement. |
| Timing | Week 8 (same week as the Mid-Project Peer Evaluation) |
| Editable after submission? | No |
| Visibility | Instructor only. Self-ratings are not shared with teammates and do not appear in the anonymized peer feedback report. |

## Pre-evaluation framing

Shown above Part A on the form.

This is your self-evaluation, the companion to the Mid-Project Peer Evaluation you are also completing this week. You rate yourself on the same four dimensions your teammates rate you on. The self-evaluation is not graded and does not affect your peer evaluation score. Your instructor uses it as a calibration signal: large gaps between how you see your contribution and how your teammates see it are worth understanding. The self-evaluation also gives you concrete material to work with in your own reflection.

Rate yourself honestly. The dimensions where you hesitate the most are usually the ones worth paying attention to. Behavioral evidence is encouraged but not required.

## Part A — Self-rating on the four BARS dimensions

Each dimension uses a 1–4 Behaviorally Anchored Rating Scale (radio buttons), followed by an optional short-text evidence field.

Section heading shown to learner: **Rate yourself on the four dimensions**

### A1. Reliability

Sub-heading question shown to learner: *Do I do what I say I will do?*

Field type: Radio buttons (single select), 1–4
Required: Yes

Options shown to learner:

| Rating | Descriptor | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | Consistently dependable | Delivers on every commitment on time or early. Shows up to meetings prepared. Notifies the team proactively if a timeline is at risk. Teammates trust this person to follow through without reminders. |
| 3 | Generally reliable | Meets most deadlines and shows up prepared for meetings. Occasionally needs a reminder but follows through when prompted. Has not caused the team to miss a milestone. |
| 2 | Inconsistently reliable | Missed a deadline or came to a meeting unprepared more than once. Required teammates to follow up or compensate. Has caused at least one delay or rework cycle for the team. |
| 1 | Unreliable | Regularly misses commitments or fails to follow through. Team cannot count on this person to deliver what they agreed to. Other members have had to absorb their responsibilities. |

Evidence field type: Long text (textarea)
Evidence field required: No

Evidence prompt shown to learner:

Optional. Briefly describe a specific commitment you made and how you followed through (or didn't).

### A2. Contribution

Sub-heading question shown to learner: *Am I doing my fair share and making the work better?*

Field type: Radio buttons (single select), 1–4
Required: Yes

Options shown to learner:

| Rating | Descriptor | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | Drives the work forward | Completes a fair share and actively looks for ways to contribute more. Volunteers for tasks when the team needs it. Work quality is strong enough that teammates rarely need to revise it. Brings ideas that improve the project. |
| 3 | Pulls their weight | Completes assigned work to an acceptable standard. Does what is asked without needing excessive direction. Work may need some editing but does not require fundamental rework. |
| 2 | Under-contributing | Needed reminders to complete assigned work or produced work that required significant revision by teammates. Did not volunteer for additional tasks even when the team was stretched. |
| 1 | Not contributing | Did not complete assigned work or relied on others to cover their responsibilities. Work product, if submitted, was unusable without being essentially redone by a teammate. |

Evidence field type: Long text (textarea)
Evidence field required: No

Evidence prompt shown to learner:

Optional. Briefly describe a specific piece of work you produced or a specific way you contributed (or did not) to the team's progress.

### A3. Constructive Engagement

Sub-heading question shown to learner: *Do I participate in a way that makes the team's work better?*

Field type: Radio buttons (single select), 1–4
Required: Yes

Options shown to learner:

| Rating | Descriptor | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | Actively improves team communication | Regularly offers specific, actionable input that makes the team's work better. Engages thoughtfully with others' ideas, building on them, asking questions, and identifying gaps. Navigates disagreements by focusing on the work rather than the person. |
| 3 | Participates constructively | Participates in team discussions and responds to feedback from others. Shares perspective when asked. Does not create communication friction. Accepts feedback without defensiveness. |
| 2 | Minimally engaged | Participates only when directly prompted or gives feedback that is vague and unhelpful (e.g., "looks good" without specifics). Does not initiate discussion or build on others' ideas. May agree with everything to avoid conflict. |
| 1 | Disengaged or counterproductive | Does not engage meaningfully in team discussions. May dominate without listening, dismiss others' contributions, or create an environment where teammates hesitate to share ideas. |

Evidence field type: Long text (textarea)
Evidence field required: No

Evidence prompt shown to learner:

Optional. Briefly describe a specific instance where your communication or feedback helped (or hindered) the team's work.

### A4. Accountability

Sub-heading question shown to learner: *Do I take responsibility for team outcomes, not just my own section?*

Field type: Radio buttons (single select), 1–4
Required: Yes

Options shown to learner:

| Rating | Descriptor | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | Owns team outcomes | Takes responsibility for the team's success, not just an individual section. Identifies problems proactively and mobilizes the team to address them. When something goes wrong, focuses on solutions rather than blame. Supports teammates who are struggling. |
| 3 | Takes personal responsibility | Takes responsibility for own work and supports the team's goals. Acknowledges when a mistake has been made. Participates in problem-solving when issues arise. |
| 2 | Deflects or waits | Tends to deflect responsibility when things go wrong or waits for others to address problems. May blame external factors (the employer, the timeline, the tools) rather than examining own role. |
| 1 | Avoids responsibility | Does not take responsibility for outcomes. Blames teammates or external factors. Does not participate in addressing team challenges. May actively undermine team cohesion through gossip, complaints, or disengagement. |

Evidence field type: Long text (textarea)
Evidence field required: No

Evidence prompt shown to learner:

Optional. Briefly describe a specific situation where you took responsibility for a team outcome (or failed to do so).

## AI use disclosure

Shown at the end of the form, before the submit button.

Field type: Radio buttons (single select), two options
Required: Yes

Prompt shown to learner:

Did you use AI assistance to help draft or refine any part of this self-evaluation?

Options shown to learner:

- No — I did not use AI on this self-evaluation.
- Yes — I used AI to help with one or more parts of this self-evaluation.

If "Yes" is selected, show a follow-up field:

Field type: Long text (textarea)
Required: Yes (only if "Yes" was selected above)

Follow-up prompt shown to learner:

In one or two sentences, describe how you used AI on this self-evaluation.
