# Kickoff Summary

*How to use this template: Complete this summary as a team within a day or two of the kickoff meeting, while the conversation is still fresh. The summary is internal. It is not sent to the employer. Its purpose is to give your team an accurate record of what the employer said so that the work you start next week builds on facts, not on what someone half-remembers. The point of the document is to surface what your team actually heard versus what it's inferring. A summary that fills every field with confident-sounding generalities does not meet the standard.*

## What the employer confirmed

The next three sections capture what the employer actually stated. If something feels true but the employer didn't say it, it belongs in the Open Questions section below, flagged as an assumption. Write as much or as little as the substance requires. A short, accurate section is better than a long, padded one.

### Confirmed scope

*The project's boundaries and focus areas as the employer described them. What's in scope? What's out? Any constraints or limitations they named?*



### Deliverables

*What the team is producing: format, length, audience, level of polish, anything else the employer specified. If the employer named the format ("a presentation," "a written report," "a set of recommendations"), capture that. If they didn't, that's an open question.*



### Success criteria

*How the employer will know the work was useful. What does a strong outcome look like to them? Capture measurable signals where they exist (e.g., "reduces onboarding time by 20%") and qualitative ones where they don't (e.g., "we can hand this to the board without rewriting it").*
