# Kickoff Meeting Script — David Park

Synthesia avatar script for the Week 3 interactive ("Reading between the lines"). The student team is meeting their employer contact, David, for a project kickoff. This is the stimulus students respond to in `page4-during-the-kickoff-interactive.html`.

## Production notes

- **Avatar**: Male, director-level warmth — mid-40s feel, neither high-energy presenter nor stiff corporate. Looking for a "I run programs for a community nonprofit and I care about this" register.
- **Background**: Office or generic professional setting. Not a boardroom. Not a green screen with a logo.
- **Pacing**: ~150 wpm. The qualifying words ("ideally," "we have some flexibility," "we're not setting that in stone") need to land naturally — neither emphasized nor buried. If the avatar pushes the qualifiers, it telegraphs the right answers; if it rushes through them, students can't catch them on the first listen.
- **Slide structure**: One continuous take is fine, or split into five slides (opening + four sections + closing) for natural pacing breaks. Slide breaks should feel like David shifting topics, not like chapter cards.
- **No on-screen text overlays.** The interactive deliberately tests listening + note-taking — captions or section labels on the video would defeat the exercise. Captions for accessibility are fine (and necessary), but treat them as a transcript, not as visual signposting.
- **CDC**: Pronounced "C-D-C" (letter by letter), not "see-dee-see."

## Script

### Opening

Thanks for making time. I'm really glad we're getting started. Before we dig into the details, let me give your team a sense of who we are and what we're hoping to do together.

### Section 1 — Project overview

We've been running these community health workshops for going on six years. By most measures, they're doing well. Attendance is solid, the feedback forms come back positive, we've built real relationships with the communities we serve. But our partner clinics have started raising something with us. They're seeing that the people who come to our workshops are learning the material in the room — they're engaged — but three months later, their actual health behaviors haven't really shifted. So we know the workshops are working as workshops. The question we have is why the learning isn't translating into change. And what we can do about it.

### Section 2 — Scope and deliverables

So what we're hoping for from your team is a research-informed set of recommendations. Look at what other organizations have tried with similar programs. Dig into the research on health behavior change, especially for the populations we serve. And come back to us with concrete suggestions we can actually put into practice. We don't need a hundred-page deliverable — honestly, we wouldn't have the time to absorb that. What would really help is a focused analysis, with clear, actionable recommendations. Something we can hand to our team and say, here's where we're going.

### Section 3 — Timeline and constraints

Timeline-wise, we have some flexibility — but let me tell you what would be ideal. Our board meets in late April, and it would be really nice to have something to share with them at that point. Even just a draft. That said, we're not setting that in stone. We'd rather you do good work than rush to make a date. On budget, this is going to need to be secondary research and analysis — we don't have the resources to fund interviews or surveys of our participants. That said, we do have some internal survey data from last year that we can share with you, if it would help your analysis.

### Section 4 — Stakeholders and context

A bit about our team and the people we serve. The workshops are run by our community health educators — eight of them, all incredibly dedicated, several of them are from the communities we serve. The curriculum itself was built in-house, four years ago now, based on CDC guidance we were using at the time. We haven't done a major refresh of it since. The one thing I really want you to keep in mind: our participants come primarily from immigrant and refugee communities. So language access matters, translation matters — but it goes beyond that. Cultural context shapes everything we do. How people understand health, who they trust, how decisions get made in families. That's the world this work lives in.

### Closing

I'll pause there — that's the broad shape of it. Take your time digging in, and let's reconnect once you've had a chance to look at the materials.

## What the script is testing (for reference — don't include in the video)

Each question in the interactive maps to specific phrases students need to catch by ear:

| Question | Listening target | Key phrases in the script |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 — Core problem | The problem is behavior change, not attendance or quality | "attendance is solid," "feedback forms come back positive," "the learning isn't translating into change" |
| Q2 — Deliverable | Focused recommendations, not a long report | "we don't need a hundred-page deliverable," "focused analysis, with clear, actionable recommendations" |
| Q3 — Deadline | Soft target, not firm | "ideally," "we're not setting that in stone," "we'd rather you do good work than rush" |
| Q4 — Research scope | Secondary only — no primary research | "secondary research and analysis," "don't have the resources to fund interviews or surveys" |
| Q5 — Cultural context | Central factor, not just translation | "translation matters — but it goes beyond that," "cultural context shapes everything we do," "how people understand health, who they trust" |

If you adjust the script during recording, make sure the bolded phrases still land — the feedback strings in the interactive quote them directly.
