# Video Script: Not a report, a story

Here is the most common version of a team presentation. The team walks on, introduces themselves, and says, "Today we'll walk you through what we did over the past fifteen weeks." Then the presentation proceeds in order. Week one, research. Week two, stakeholder interviews. Week three, analysis. At the end, there are some recommendations.

Technically, this is a presentation. The audience has heard about the work. The problem is that the audience already knew most of what was described, and the one thing they didn't know, what the team concluded and what to do about it, arrives in the last two minutes when attention is lowest.

Now here is a version that works differently. A team presenting a donor engagement analysis for a nonprofit. They walk on and open with, "Your donor retention has been dropping for three years, and the reason we found was not the one most of the board has been assuming." That is the first sentence. The rest of the presentation is the team explaining how they arrived there and what the nonprofit should do about it.

The audience hears that opening and leans in. The second version starts with the thing the audience actually came to hear. The first version buries it.

Same shift on a process improvement project. The wrong version is, "We looked at your invoice approval workflow and found some opportunities." The right version is, "Your invoice approvals are taking twice as long as they should, and we can show you where the delay is." The difference is where the presentation points the audience's attention in the first ten seconds.

One more. A team presenting a community outreach plan. The wrong version is, "Our team has developed recommendations for expanding your outreach." The right version names a specific claim the recommendations rest on. "Your outreach is reaching the wrong audience, and here is the audience we think it should reach instead."

Three presentations, same pattern. The employer does not need a report of what your team did. They need a story about what your team found, what it means, and what to do about it. A report lists activities. A story makes a claim and defends it.

That is the move this week is asking you to make.
