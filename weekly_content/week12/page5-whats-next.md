# What's next

The deliverable is substantially done. If your team used this week's production block well, next week is not another production week. Next week is where the package gets a final polish pass, a whole-document quality-review pass, and goes out the door.

---

## Finalize and submit

Next week's work is lighter than this week's by design. The shape of the Final Deliverables is already there. The remaining moves are a final read-through, a consistency pass on formatting and language, a whole-document AI quality-review pass on the assembled package, and the submission itself. Final Deliverables carry the largest grade weight of any single deliverable in the course.

Teams who leave structural work to next week tend to submit something rougher than the version sitting in their drive on Monday morning. The professional default is the opposite pattern: enter the final week in submission-ready shape, and let the week be for verification and polish.

---

## The optional Final Check-In

The optional Final Check-In with the employer is available next week. It is the last employer touchpoint before submission.

What the Final Check-In is for: a short conversation to ask targeted questions about anything still unclear after the Near-Final Review. Whether a specific paragraph lands the way the team intends. Whether a particular recommendation is pitched at the right level. A few narrow questions the team cannot answer from inside the room. That is what it is for.

What it is not for: a second full review of the deliverable. The employer's substantive feedback already came through the Near-Final Review. Asking them to re-review at this stage is not the use of their time the meeting is designed for.

Skip the Final Check-In if your team does not have specific questions that the meeting could answer. Take it if you do. Bring a short list of questions and keep the session tight.

---

## How to prepare

- Enter next week with the deliverable at Near-Final Review quality, updated with the feedback items your team triaged this week and addressed in the production block.
- Keep the Change Log current through this week. Every Near-Final Review feedback item should be documented with the team's decision and, where action was taken, where it shows up in the deliverable. Reconstructing this after the fact is slower and less accurate than entering it as the work is done.
- The presentation outline from this week is a working draft. Leave it where your team can find it. Rehearsal and refinement happen in a couple of weeks, and the checkpoint submission is at that point.
- Submit Reflection #4 before the end of the week.
- Track AI use across this week's production so the AI disclosure on next week's Final Deliverables submission reflects what actually happened rather than getting reconstructed the night before.

---

## Looking ahead

Two weeks from now, the focus shifts to the presentation. That week is when the outline your team drafted this week gets refined, rehearsed, and adjusted based on peer feedback. The outline itself is submitted as a checkpoint at that point.

The End-of-Project Peer Evaluation also opens in that timeframe. Like the Mid-Project Peer Evaluation earlier in the course, it is an individual submission that evaluates your teammates' contributions across the project. You will have the framework in hand before it opens.
