# Video Script: The revision that made it worse

A consulting team is two days from a client delivery. The draft is strong. In a final review meeting, a partner suggests restructuring the audience segmentation. It would take a full day of rework, but the new version is slightly sharper.

The team takes the suggestion.

The new segmentation works in Section Two, where the change started. In Section Four, it contradicts a claim the team made earlier. The executive summary still uses the old structure. A chart in the appendix references segments that technically no longer exist. Each of these is a small problem on its own. The delivery goes out with five small problems that were not there the day before.

The cost of a late-stage change is rarely just the rework hours the team sees. It is the rework plus the propagation into every section that used to reference the old version, which nobody thinks to check until the next full read.

When your team triages the Near-Final Review feedback this week, that propagation cost is what to hold against each proposed change.
