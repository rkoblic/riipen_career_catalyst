# Video Script: Knowing when to stop

This week, your team faces a question that gets sharper the closer you get to delivery: when is the work done?

In school, the deadline usually settles it. You revise until time runs out, then hand in your best version. More work late in the process tends to mean a better submission.

Professional work doesn't always behave that way. Close to delivery, every change carries some risk of breaking what already works. A fix can introduce a new problem. A polish pass can destabilize a section that was already strong. So the question needs to shift from "how can I make this better?" to "is this change worth the risk?" That's the question a consulting team asks before client delivery. It's the question an editor asks before a manuscript goes to print. It's the call your team is about to make on a real deliverable.

You have the employer's feedback on your near-final deliverables, and one last push to get them across the line. This week, your team decides which pieces of feedback to act on and which to set aside. By the end of the week, the deliverable is done because the team made the call, not because time ran out.

That's the professional practice this week is asking you to run.
