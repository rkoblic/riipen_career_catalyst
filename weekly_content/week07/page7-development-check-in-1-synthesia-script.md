# Development Check-In Pushback Scripts — David Park

Synthesia avatar scripts for the Week 7 interactive ("When feedback catches you off guard"). Same recurring character from the Week 3 kickoff and Week 5 Direction Check. This is Development Check-In #1, where David flags an unexpected piece of history — the team has just made a peer navigator program their headline recommendation, and David knows Bridgeway already tried one and it didn't work.

**Two short videos, not one.** The student watches Part 1 (David's flag), works through Q1 (Listen) and Q2 (Clarify), then watches Part 2 (David's elaboration after the team's clarifying question), then works through Q3 (Acknowledge), Q4 (teammate moment), and Q5 (Decide later → action item).

## Production notes

- **Same avatar as Weeks 3 and 5.** Continuity carries the arc — David is the team's primary employer contact across the project. Reuse the avatar, voice, and background from `Kickoff Meeting with David Park.mp4` and `Direction Check with David Park.mp4`.
- **Register**: Slightly warmer than the Direction Check. David isn't redirecting here — he's *flagging* something, almost doing the team a favor by surfacing it. The opening "I'd rather you hear it from me than find it later" should sound like a friendly heads-up, not a verdict. He gets more measured in Part 2 when he's giving the actual history.
- **Pacing**: ~150 wpm. The pauses in Part 1 carry the emotional weight. "I'm not going to say more than that right now..." needs real breath — it's a deliberate choice to hold back, and the avatar needs to convey that as a choice, not as a fade. Same for Part 2's "OK, sure. So..." — David is composing his thoughts, not stalling.
- **In media res — no greeting, no wrap-up.** Both videos open with David in a listening posture (a small beat, slight nod) and end on the last word of the script. No "I'll let you respond" or "let me know what you think." The silences after each end are where the student is meant to be doing the work.
- **Part 2 continuity.** Same room, same posture, same lighting as Part 1 — it's the same meeting. The student should not feel like they're entering a new scene.
- **Tone check before committing.** Generate Section 1 of Part 1 first as a 20-second test. Watch for: does the avatar sound like a director giving a heads-up (good), like a verdict (bad), or like passive-aggression (bad)? Adjust voice/avatar selection before generating both videos.
- **No on-screen text overlays.** Captions for accessibility are fine; no section labels.

## Script — Part 1 (The flag)

*Plays after the context card, before Q1.*

### Section 1 — Appreciation and the turn

Thank you, that's really helpful. And honestly, a lot of this is tracking with where I thought you'd land.

### Section 2 — The flag

But I want to flag something on the peer navigator piece — because I'd rather you hear it from me than find it later. We actually ran a version of this. About two years ago. A peer navigator pilot. It didn't go the way we hoped, and we wound it down after about ten months.

### Section 3 — The restraint

I'm not going to say more than that right now... because I'm curious what your team has been thinking. I don't want to color your direction before I've heard it. But I do want you to factor it in. Before you commit to this as a headline recommendation.

## Script — Part 2 (The history)

*Plays after Q2 (clarify), before Q3 (acknowledge). The interactive frames this as "Your team asks a clarifying question. David takes a beat, then responds." Regardless of what the student typed for Q2, Part 2 plays as David's response.*

### Section 1 — What went wrong

OK, sure. So... we had problems with how we recruited and trained the navigators. The training wasn't deep enough for what we were asking people to do. And we lost trust with two of our partner clinics over it — they felt like we'd sent people into their spaces who weren't ready.

### Section 2 — The qualifier

I think the model can work. But you need to know that history is there.

## What's different from the text version

The source markdown read cleanly on the page but used phrasing that would sound stiff aloud. Adjustments:

- "I want to flag something on the peer navigator piece, because I'd rather you hear it from me than find it later. We actually ran a version of this about two years ago." → broke into shorter sentences with an em-dash pause and a sentence break: "...because I'd rather you hear it from me than find it later. We actually ran a version of this. About two years ago." Gives the avatar room to pace it as a reluctant disclosure.
- "I'm not going to say more than that right now, because I'm curious what your team has been thinking, and I don't want to color your direction before I've heard it." → ellipsis pause and a sentence break: "I'm not going to say more than that right now... because I'm curious what your team has been thinking. I don't want to color your direction before I've heard it."
- "before you commit to building this out as a headline recommendation" → "before you commit to this as a headline recommendation." Dropped "building this out as" — written construction, not spoken.
- "The previous pilot ran into problems with how the navigators were recruited and trained, and Bridgeway lost trust with two of the partner clinics over it." → expanded into more vivid, more spoken language: "we had problems with how we recruited and trained the navigators. The training wasn't deep enough for what we were asking people to do. And we lost trust with two of our partner clinics over it — they felt like we'd sent people into their spaces who weren't ready." Adds texture David would actually give and makes the partner clinic detail more memorable for Q3.

The model responses and self-check rubrics in the interactive markdown have been updated to quote the new phrasing.
