# Video Script: Structural feedback vs. line editing

When you read a colleague's piece, the first instinct is usually to fix the small stuff. A clunky sentence, a word you'd change, a transition that's off. That's line editing, and it's the least useful thing you can do at this stage.

Structural feedback asks a different question. Does this piece do what it's supposed to do? Does it have a clear claim? Does the evidence actually support it? Would someone who doesn't know the project follow it?

Here's the difference. A teammate's section on market positioning in a customer onboarding redesign opens with three paragraphs of company background, then makes its single real claim in paragraph four. A line editor would fix the grammar in those opening paragraphs. A structural reviewer would flag the real issue, which is that the section buries its main point under setup the reader doesn't need. That's not a prose problem. It's a structure problem, and polishing the sentences wouldn't fix it.

Same pattern in a different format. A teammate's slide deck for a digital skills program evaluation has a slide titled "Learner outcomes" with five statistics on it. The data is on the slide. The interpretation is not. A line editor might suggest cleaner phrasing for the title. A structural reviewer would say the slide is missing its takeaway, and the fix is a title that says what the numbers mean, not what they are.

Structural feedback comes first. Once the logic holds and the piece does its job, that's when you read for language.
