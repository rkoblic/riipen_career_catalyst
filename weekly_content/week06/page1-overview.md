# From planning to production

Two things this week. First, close out the Discovery phase with Reflection #2 — look back at the research, the Direction Check, and everything that brought you to this point, and pull out what's worth carrying forward. Then start production: get the team set up, get the document set up, and start drafting your section.

---

## What's due this week

**Reflection #2** is the only graded deliverable this week (individual submission, approximately 5% of your final grade as part of the Reflection Portfolio). There's no employer touchpoint this week, which means your attention goes entirely to the reflection, the modules, and your production work.

Complete the reflection early in the week, before you start production. It reflects on the Discovery phase that's now behind you, and finishing it first lets you turn the page cleanly into production work.

---

## Suggested order of completion

The order of this week matters. Reflection #2 comes first because it closes a chapter. The production modules come next, sequenced for specific moments in the drafting process. Then you research and draft.

1. **Reflection #2** — First. Looks back at the Discovery phase: the research, the Direction Check, the moments that tested your judgment. Done early, it gives you a clean break between phases.

2. **Starting production as a team** — Before anyone opens the shared document. Covers the setup decisions your team needs to make together first: document structure, section ownership, formatting standards, and terminology. Skipping this creates problems that are hard to fix later.

3. **Formatting professional deliverables** — Immediately after setting up the workspace, before any content is added. Apply these standards to the template first, then build.

4. **Making progress under uncertainty** — Before you start your independent research or sit down to draft. Addresses the most common thing that stalls production: not knowing how to move forward when you don't have everything you need.

5. **Researching your assigned piece** — Once you know which section is yours, work through what research it actually needs and where to find it. This is the bridge between team-level Discovery research and your individual draft.

6. **From research to draft** — After your research is done, before you try to write. Covers the step between notes and prose that most people skip, which is where most production stalls.

7. **Navigating disagreement** — Read this before you hit a moment that calls for it. Most teams run into some kind of disagreement once they start co-writing (about direction, about quality, about who's doing what), and a few don't. Either way, it's worth knowing how to handle one before you're in it.

After the modules: get started. The goal is to end the week with a named section drafted to a point where you can talk through it in a room.
