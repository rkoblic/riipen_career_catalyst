# From planning to development

Two things this week. First, close out the Discovery phase with Reflection #2. Look back at the research, the Direction Check, and everything that brought you to this point, and pull out what's worth carrying forward. Then start development: revisit what you're building toward, get the team set up, and start drafting your first piece.

---

## What's due this week

**Reflection #2** is the only graded deliverable this week (individual submission, approximately 5% of your final grade as part of the Reflection Portfolio). There's no employer touchpoint this week, which means your attention goes entirely to the reflection, the modules, and your development work.

Complete the reflection early in the week, before you start development. It reflects on the Discovery phase that's now behind you, and finishing it first lets you turn the page cleanly into development work.

---

## Suggested order of completion

The order of this week matters. Reflection #2 comes first because it closes a chapter. Revisiting the Final Deliverables assignment comes next because it shapes the development decisions your team is about to make. The development modules are sequenced after that, each one timed for a specific moment in the drafting process.

1. **Reflection #2.** Looks back at the Discovery phase: the research, the Direction Check, the moments that tested your judgment. Done early, it gives you a clean break between phases.

2. **Revisiting the Final Deliverable.** You reviewed this at the start of the course as part of your syllabus orientation. Now that you have a project, a direction, and a team, re-read the assignment and rubric before your team starts making development decisions. It's the work everything from here on is building toward.

3. **Starting development as a team.** Before anyone opens the shared workspace. Covers the setup decisions your team needs to make together first: component ownership, structure, formatting standards, and terminology. The setup work here prevents problems that are harder to fix later.

4. **Formatting professional deliverables.** Immediately after setting up the workspace, before any content goes in. Apply these standards to the template first, then build.

5. **Making progress under uncertainty.** Before you start your independent research or sit down to draft. Addresses the most common thing that stalls development: not knowing how to move forward when you don't have everything you need.

6. **Preparing your assigned piece.** Once you know which piece is yours, work through what it actually needs — research, synthesis, analysis, or some mix — and get the material ready to draft from. This is the bridge between team-level Discovery work and your individual development work.

7. **From research to draft.** After your research is done, before you try to write. Covers the step between notes and prose that most people skip, which is where most development tends to stall.

8. **Navigating disagreement.** Read this before you hit a moment that calls for it. Most teams run into some kind of disagreement once development starts (about direction, about quality, about who's doing what), and it's worth knowing how to handle one before you're in it.

After the modules: get started. The goal is to end the week with your assigned piece developed to a point where you can talk through it in a room.
