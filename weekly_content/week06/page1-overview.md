# From planning to development

This week is a hinge. Close out the Discovery phase with Reflection #2 — look back at the research, the Direction Check, and everything that brought you to this point, and pull out what's worth carrying forward. Then start development: revisit what you're building toward, get the team set up, and start drafting your first piece.

---

## What's due this week

**Reflection #2** is the only graded deliverable this week (individual submission, 5% of your final grade as part of the Reflection Portfolio). There's no employer touchpoint this week, which means your attention goes entirely to the reflection, the modules, and your development work.

Complete the reflection early in the week, before you start development.

---

## Suggested order of completion

The order of this week matters. Reflection #2 first (it closes a chapter), Revisiting the Project Deliverable next (it shapes the decisions your team is about to make). The development modules are sequenced after that, each one timed for a specific moment in the drafting process.

1. **Reflection #2.** Looks back at the moments in Discovery that tested your judgment. Done early, it gives you a clean break between phases.

2. **Revisiting the Project Deliverable.** You looked at this during syllabus orientation. Re-read it now that you have a project and team. It's what every development decision builds toward.

3. **Starting development as a team.** Before anyone opens the shared workspace. Covers setup decisions to make together: component ownership, structure, formatting standards, terminology. Settle these now or fix them later.

4. **Formatting professional deliverables.** Right after workspace setup, before any content goes in. Apply the standards to the template first, then build.

5. **Making progress under uncertainty.** Before independent research or drafting. Addresses what stalls most development: not knowing how to move forward without everything you need.

6. **Preparing your assigned piece.** Once you know which piece is yours, work through what it actually needs (research, synthesis, analysis) and get the material ready to draft from. The bridge between team Discovery and your individual work.

7. **From research to draft.** After research, before drafting. Covers the step between notes and prose that most people skip. It's where most drafts stall.

8. **Navigating disagreement.** Read this before you hit a moment that calls for it. Most teams run into disagreement once development starts: about direction, quality, or who's doing what.

After the modules: get started. The goal is to end the week with your assigned piece developed to a point where you can talk through it in a room.
