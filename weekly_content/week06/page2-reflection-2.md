# Reflection #2

Discovery is done. Reflection #2 closes the phase. Before you turn into development, take stock of what those weeks taught you.

Where Reflection #1 looked at what it was like to enter a new professional situation, Reflection #2 looks at what it was like to do your first real piece of professional work in it.

---

This is your second DEAL reflection, an individual submission graded as part of the Reflection Portfolio. The framework and rubric are the same as Reflection #1. What's different this time is what you have to draw on, and how deep the examination needs to go.

---

## The prompt

Choose a specific moment from the last few weeks where the path forward was unclear and you had to decide how to act without complete information.

The most likely anchor is the Context Analysis & Project Plan. Your first major graded team deliverable required navigating uncertainty throughout. Other candidates: the research process, a team decision under pressure, the Direction Check, or a team dynamic that surfaced as you worked toward submission.

Use the DEAL framework to describe what happened, examine what your response to that ambiguity reveals about how you take initiative and navigate uncertainty, and articulate what you've learned about your own approach when the answer isn't obvious.

What matters is that you were in the experience, not observing it.

---

## What changes for your second reflection

You've worked through the framework once already, so the mechanics aren't new this time. The expectation is that you use the space that frees up to push the examination deeper.

The rubric is the same: Descriptive Specificity, Critical Examination, Personal Accountability, and Articulation of Learning, equally weighted. What's different is that your instructor reads this with your first reflection in view. Meeting the standard means going further than you did the first time.

The most common plateau: holding the same depth in the Examine stage because it was sufficient before. It was sufficient as a first attempt. This is a second attempt. Push further than felt necessary last time.

Personal accountability is where most first reflections fall short. That means examining your own role, decisions, and assumptions rather than narrating events from outside. If you placed yourself inside the experience in your first reflection, go deeper. If you didn't, this is the time to correct that.

---

## Using AI

The boundary on AI is the same as Reflection #1: it can't supply the experience, the examination, or the learning. A reflection that could have been written by any learner in any project hasn't met the standard, regardless of what tools were used.

Where AI helps is in deepening the Examine stage:

- **Surface unnamed assumptions.** Describe your experience to an AI tool and ask: "What assumptions might I be making that I haven't explicitly named?" Use what it identifies as a prompt for your own thinking, not as conclusions to copy.
- **Push past surface-level examination.** Share a draft Examine section and ask: "Where am I still on the surface here? What's a harder question I could be asking myself?" The friction is the point.
- **Anticipate a skeptical reader.** Ask the AI what questions a skeptical reader would have. Then go back into the draft and address them. Don't explain yourself to the AI.

The intellectual work is yours. AI helps you see where you haven't gone far enough.

---

## Submission

**Length:** 800–1200 words. Under 700 words rarely achieves sufficient depth in the Examine stage. Over 1,400 words usually indicates the description is too broad. Pick one experience and go deeper, not wider.

**Structure:** Use the three DEAL stages as organizing sections. Headers are encouraged but not required; what matters is that a reader can tell where description ends and examination begins.

**Submission:** Individually through the Riipen platform.

**AI disclosure:** If you used AI at any point in your reflection process, include a brief description of what you used and how. If you did not use AI, a one-sentence statement to that effect is sufficient. Disclosure is required either way.
