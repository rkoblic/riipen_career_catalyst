# Final Deliverables submission

This page covers what to submit, how to verify the package is ready, what the rubric assesses, and how to use the Change Log and AI disclosure on this submission. Read it before the production block, not while you're assembling.

---

## The home stretch

The last week before a professional deliverable ships is predictable in a specific way. Teams that have been building for months arrive at the end and suddenly see all the things they wish they'd done differently. The gaps look larger than they did two weeks ago. The version in the drive looks worn.

What this moment calls for is not more revision. It calls for judgment.

[VIDEO: Trust your process]

The triage framework from last week still applies here: every remaining item is either a change that has to happen before submission, something to acknowledge in the Change Log without changing the deliverable, or something genuinely out of scope to document and move on from. The distinction that matters most in the home stretch is between changes that would break something if you don't make them, and changes that would be nice to make if time permitted. The first category is small. The second is large, and acting on it this late tends to introduce problems faster than it resolves them.

The version your team is submitting has gone through the Near-Final Review, been revised based on that feedback, reviewed again by your team, and revised again. Trust what that process produced. The remaining moves are a final read-through, a consistency pass, and submission.

---

## What this submission is

**The Final Deliverables package includes:**

- The deliverable as specified in your project brief, all components assembled into a single submission
- The Change Log documenting feedback received across the project and how your team addressed it
- Your AI disclosure statement

This is the work product your team delivers to the employer. The rubric assesses the package as a whole, not just the deliverable content. The Change Log and the AI disclosure are part of what's evaluated.

**What the rubric assesses**

Five dimensions with the following weights:

| Dimension | Weight | What it evaluates |
|---|---|---|
| Deliverable Quality | 25% | Completeness, scope alignment, depth of development |
| Analytical Rigor | 25% | Evidence base, soundness of reasoning, synthesis |
| Actionability | 25% | Whether the employer can act on the work |
| Communication | 15% | Clarity, organization, professional presentation |
| Responsiveness to Feedback | 10% | Documented iteration based on employer and peer input |

The three core dimensions (Deliverable Quality, Analytical Rigor, and Actionability) carry equal weight because none succeeds without the others. A deliverable can be analytically rigorous but impractical, or practically useful but thin on evidence. The rubric treats these as genuinely interdependent.

**Grade weight**

The Final Deliverables carry the largest grade weight of any single submission in the course. No individual reflection, peer evaluation, or other checkpoint matches it. That weight is also why the weeks since the Near-Final Review have been focused on getting the package right rather than adding new content.

## Final Deliverable Checklist

Run through this during the internal review gate, not while you're still assembling. The checklist is a verification pass, not a production tool.

- [ ] **All deliverable components are present.** Every component specified in the project brief is included in the submission.
- [ ] **Formatting is consistent across sections.** Fonts, headings, spacing, and visual style match throughout the entire package, not only in recently revised sections.
- [ ] **Terminology and voice read as one document.** The same concept uses the same word throughout. The prose doesn't shift registers between sections written by different team members.
- [ ] **Errors are caught.** Typos, broken cross-references, and orphaned placeholders have been found on a final read.
- [ ] **Every rubric dimension has visible evidence.** Don't leave it to the evaluator to infer. If Actionability is a dimension, check that implementation guidance is specific and visible. If Responsiveness to Feedback is a dimension, check that the Change Log is complete and attached.
- [ ] **The Change Log is complete.** Every substantive feedback item from employer touchpoints and draft reviews is documented with the team's response or rationale for not acting on it.
- [ ] **The AI disclosure statement is accurate.** It reflects what actually happened during production, not a reconstruction from memory.
- [ ] **The submitted version is the correct one.** Confirm as a team that what's being uploaded is the version everyone agreed is final. Accidental submission of an earlier draft is more common than teams expect.

## About the Change Log

Your team started the Change Log at the Direction Check and has been updating it through every round of employer feedback since. By this point it should be a substantive record spanning the full project, not a list assembled in the last two days.

The Responsiveness to Feedback dimension of the rubric is assessed directly through the Change Log. Without it, that dimension scores as Not Acceptable, which eliminates 10% of the Final Deliverables grade regardless of how strong the deliverable itself is.

A complete Change Log at this stage documents three things for each piece of substantive feedback received:

- What the feedback was and where it came from
- What the team decided to do in response: action taken, deliberate decision not to act, or deferred as out of scope
- Where the change appears in the deliverable, if action was taken

The most important Change Log entries to check now are the ones from the Near-Final Review. That feedback was the most recent and the most consequential before final submission. If those entries are missing or thin, add them before submitting.

## AI guidance

At this stage, AI is for checking, not creating. Appropriate uses include: formatting consistency passes, coherence checking across sections, prose tightening, error catching, and running the fresh reader or weakest-link prompts on assembled sections you haven't looked at in a while. Using AI to check the Change Log for completeness is a particularly good application: "here are the feedback items I've logged; what looks thin or missing?"

What AI cannot do on this submission: generate the analysis, findings, or recommendations that make up your deliverable. The Final Presentation in the closing weeks of the course is a live defend-your-work moment. The employer will ask questions about specific sections, specific recommendations, and specific evidence. If AI generated content your team cannot explain in that conversation, submitting it now creates a more visible problem two weeks from now, not later.

AI-assisted editing and quality review are appropriate. AI-generated content substituting for your team's analysis is not.

**AI disclosure** is required on this submission. Use the disclosure your team has been tracking across the production weeks rather than reconstructing it. Include it as part of the submitted package.
