# Video Script: Trust your process

The impulse to make a significant change the night before submission is predictable. Every team feels it. The version in the drive doesn't look the way it did at the start of the project. It looks worn in. The gaps are visible in a way they weren't before.

What that impulse misses is the cost of acting on it. A structural change at this stage touches everything connected to it. The executive summary. The implementation section. The references. Every section that was built on top of the thing being changed. The change itself might be right. What fails is the propagation: the six other places that still refer to the old version, quietly, until the team doesn't have time to find them.

The version your team is about to submit has gone through the Near-Final Review and been revised based on that feedback. It has been reviewed again by your team. It has been revised again. That is a lot of process on one deliverable. The Near-Final Review and the weeks of revision that followed it exist specifically to get the package to where it is right now.

Trust what that process produced.
