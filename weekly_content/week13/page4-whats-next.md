# What's next

The Refinement phase closes with this week's submission. The next phase is Presentation: the shift from finishing the work to telling the story of it.

---

## Weeks 14 and 15

**The coming week** is presentation-refinement week. The outline your team drafted earlier gets refined and rehearsed, and the Presentation Outline is submitted as a checkpoint. The End-of-Project Peer Evaluation also opens in this period. Reflection #5 drafting begins. Reading the prompt now puts you ahead of that deadline.

**The final week** is the Final Presentation and the course wrap: the live presentation to the employer, the End-of-Project Peer Evaluation submission, and Reflection #5. The Final Presentation is the only formal presentation in the closing phase of the course and the one live defend-your-work moment where the employer can ask about any part of the work your team produced.

---

## Previewing Reflection #5

[**Reflection #5 prompt**](URL_TBD) is linked here so you can read it now, two weeks before it's due.

Reflection #5 asks you to step back over the full course arc and examine the most significant shift in your understanding or capability across the project. That kind of reflection is easier to write with specific evidence (particular moments, decisions, and surprises) than it is to reconstruct from a general impression after the project is complete.

The best time to start noticing is now, while the project is still live and the work is fresh. Three questions to carry into the coming weeks:

- What can you do now that you couldn't do at the start of the project? Not a project description. A specific skill or judgment you didn't have in the first few weeks.
- Where did you change your mind about how professional work gets done? The shift itself, and what caused it.
- What moment across the project would you tell someone about if they asked what this course was actually like?

You don't need to start drafting. The noticing begins the moment you know what the prompt is asking for.

## Previewing the End-of-Project Peer Evaluation

[**End-of-Project Peer Evaluation instructions**](URL_TBD) are linked here so you can read them before the evaluation opens.

The End-of-Project Peer Evaluation uses the same framework as the Mid-Project Peer Evaluation earlier in the course: behaviorally anchored scales across the same four Teamwork subskill dimensions. Where the mid-project evaluation was formative (feedback to act on with several weeks of the project remaining), this one is summative. The ratings and qualitative feedback constitute a final account of each team member's contributions and close the peer feedback loop for the project.

Peer evaluation quality depends on behavioral evidence. General impressions are hard to translate into useful ratings or specific qualitative feedback. The remaining weeks are a good time to notice specific moments: a teammate who stepped up in a difficult stretch, a pattern you've observed in how someone handles feedback, a contribution that made a visible difference. The more specific the evidence, the more useful the evaluation.

## How to prepare for the coming week

- **Submit the Final Deliverables this week.** The presentation work in the coming weeks assumes the deliverable is done.
- **Keep the Change Log current** through this week's revisions and the Final Check-In if held. The Change Log is referenced in the Final Presentation's defend-your-work moment. Having it current and complete matters through the close of the course.
- **Read the Reflection #5 prompt** linked above. You don't need to start drafting. The noticing begins the moment you know what the prompt is asking for.
- **Read the End-of-Project Peer Evaluation instructions** linked above. Begin noticing specific behavioral evidence about your teammates while the project is still live.
- **Confirm presentation logistics with the employer** (format, date, time, and attendees) if you haven't already. The Final Check-In page covers the logistics communication.
- **Have your presentation outline from last week ready to pick up.** Refinement and rehearsal begin in the coming week, and starting from a draft outline is faster than starting from a blank page.

## Closing the Refinement phase

This week closes the Refinement phase. The Development phase was about building. The Refinement phase was about making what you built ready for a professional audience. The Presentation phase that follows is about something different again: translating the work: turning what your team made into a story the employer can receive, remember, and use.

That translation is not a summary of the deliverable. It's a structured account of what your team found, what your team decided, and why the work matters for the employer's context. The deliverable already exists. The coming weeks are about how you present it.
