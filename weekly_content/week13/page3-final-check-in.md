# Final Check-In (optional)

The Final Check-In is an optional meeting available this week. Whether to hold it is a professional judgment call, not a default. This page covers how to make that decision and how to communicate with the employer about presentation logistics regardless of whether the meeting takes place.

---

## What this touchpoint is (and isn't)

The Final Check-In is a 60-minute hold available for teams with targeted remaining questions about their deliverable. It is the last employer touchpoint before the Final Presentation.

What it is for: a short, focused conversation (probably 30 minutes of the 60-minute hold) where your team brings specific questions that the employer's input would resolve and that the team cannot answer from inside the room. Whether a particular recommendation is pitched at the right level for their context. Whether a specific section answers what they actually needed to know.

What it is not for: a second full review of the deliverable, a substitute for the Near-Final Review that already happened, or a meeting to relieve the team's anxiety about submission. The employer's substantive feedback came through the Near-Final Review. This meeting is for the narrow set of questions that couldn't be answered then and still can't be answered now.

Cancelling a meeting you don't need is not laziness. It is respect for the employer's time, and it's the kind of professional judgment the course has been building toward across every employer touchpoint. The employers working with Riipen teams are investing significant time. Using that time well includes knowing when a meeting would not be the best use of it.

---

## How to make the hold/cancel decision

**The substantive test**

A substantive question is one where the employer's answer would change something in the deliverable before submission. A reassurance question is one where the answer would make the team feel better but the deliverable would ship the same either way.

Hold the meeting if your team has substantive questions. Cancel if the only questions are reassurance questions.

**What your Change Log and internal review gate tell you**

If your team's final internal review gate flagged specific gaps that require employer input to resolve, you likely have substantive questions. If the review gate confirmed the work is ready and the Change Log captures the Near-Final Review feedback and responses, you probably don't.

Check the Change Log entries from the Near-Final Review. Anywhere the team logged "we're not sure how to address this" or "deferred, needs employer clarification," that's a potential question for the Final Check-In. Anywhere the entry reads "addressed in section X" or "decided not to change because Y," the question is already resolved.

**When to cancel even if you have questions**

If a remaining question can be resolved by the team without the employer (a judgment call about language, a scope decision, a formatting question), resolve it internally. The Final Check-In is for questions only the employer can answer. Everything else belongs to the team.

---

## If the Final Check-In is held

**Suggested time breakdown for approximately 30 minutes:**

- Brief status update (about 5 minutes): Let the employer know the package is submission-ready except for the specific items you're bringing to the meeting. Keep this short. The employer has already reviewed the near-final package and doesn't need a full recap.
- Remaining questions (about 15 to 20 minutes): Work through the questions in priority order. If the employer's answer to the first question resolves something you thought was a second question, move on. Don't over-structure this part.
- Presentation logistics (about 5 minutes): Confirm the format, date, time, and attendees for the Final Presentation if you haven't already done this asynchronously.

**After the check-in**

Debrief immediately. Update the Change Log with each piece of substantive feedback from the meeting, including the decisions the team made in response. Apply the triage framework your team used after the Near-Final Review: requires action before submission, acknowledge in the Change Log without changing the deliverable, or out of scope. Given how close to the submission deadline this meeting typically falls, the "out of scope" category may carry more than usual. That's a legitimate call to make explicitly.

---

## Communicating about presentation logistics

This communication is non-negotiable regardless of whether the Final Check-In is held. The presentation work in the coming weeks cannot proceed without format, timing, and attendees confirmed with the employer.

**What to confirm:**

- **Presentation format:** live in person, live remote, recorded, or hybrid
- **Date and time:** confirmed date and start time, including time zone
- **Duration:** how long the presentation is expected to run, including time for Q&A
- **Attendees:** who from the employer organization will be present; any additional stakeholders who will attend

**Tone:** Administrative and forward-looking. This is a logistics message, not a check-in. If the Final Check-In was cancelled, a brief note confirming the team is on track is appropriate. It signals that the cancellation was deliberate, not an oversight.

**Template:**

> **Subject:** Final Presentation logistics — [Team/Project name]
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> We're on track for submitting our Final Deliverables this week and wanted to confirm a few logistics for the Final Presentation. Could you let us know the preferred format (in person, remote, or hybrid), the confirmed date and time, expected duration, and who from your team will be attending?
>
> We're looking forward to sharing the finished work. Please let us know if there's anything you need from us before then.
>
> [Team sign-off]
