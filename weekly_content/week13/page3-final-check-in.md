# Final Check-In

This week's standing biweekly check-in with the employer is the last one before the Final Presentation in Week 15. It is a 30-minute hold. This page covers how to decide what to do with the slot (hold the meeting, ask your remaining questions asynchronously, or cancel), how to run the meeting if you hold it, and how to communicate with the employer about presentation logistics either way.

---

## What this touchpoint is (and isn't)

This is the same biweekly hold your team has been using throughout the project. The difference with this one is timing: it falls during the final push to submission, and it's the last scheduled meeting before the Final Presentation.

Use it for specific questions that only the employer can answer: whether a particular recommendation is pitched at the right level for their context, whether a specific section answers what they needed to know, anything else the team can't resolve from inside the room.

Don't use it as a second full review of the deliverable or as a substitute for the Near-Final Review. The employer's substantive feedback came through that review. This meeting is for the narrow set of questions that couldn't be answered then and still can't be answered now.

---

## How to decide what to do

**The substantive test**

A substantive question is one where the employer's answer would change something in the deliverable before submission. A reassurance question is one where the answer would make the team feel better but the deliverable would ship the same either way.

You have three options for this slot:

- **Hold the meeting** if your team has substantive questions that benefit from a live conversation.
- **Send your questions asynchronously** (email or your usual channel) if you have substantive questions but a meeting isn't worth the time. This works well when the answers are short or yes/no.
- **Cancel** if the only questions are reassurance questions.

**What your Change Log and internal review gate tell you**

If your team's final internal review gate flagged specific gaps that require employer input to resolve, you likely have substantive questions. If the review gate confirmed the work is ready and the Change Log captures the Near-Final Review feedback and responses, you probably don't.

Check the Change Log entries from the Near-Final Review. Anywhere the team logged "we're not sure how to address this" or "deferred, needs employer clarification," that's a potential question for this check-in. Anywhere the entry reads "addressed in section X" or "decided not to change because Y," the question is already resolved.

**When to cancel even if you have questions**

If a remaining question can be resolved by the team without the employer (a judgment call about language, a scope decision, a formatting question), resolve it internally. Both the meeting and the async option are for questions only the employer can answer.

---

## If you hold the check-in

**Suggested breakdown for the 30-minute hold:**

- Brief status update (about 5 minutes): Let the employer know the package is submission-ready except for the specific items you're bringing to the meeting. Keep this short. The employer has already reviewed the near-final package and doesn't need a full recap.
- Remaining questions (about 20 minutes): Work through the questions in priority order. If the employer's answer to the first question resolves something you thought was a second question, move on. Don't over-structure this part.
- Presentation logistics (about 5 minutes): Confirm the format, date, time, and attendees for the Final Presentation if you haven't already done this asynchronously.

**After the check-in**

Debrief immediately. Update the Change Log with each piece of substantive feedback from the meeting, including the decisions the team made in response. Apply the triage framework your team used after the Near-Final Review: requires action before submission, acknowledge in the Change Log without changing the deliverable, or out of scope. Given how close to the submission deadline this meeting falls, the "out of scope" category will carry more than usual.

---

## Communicating about presentation logistics

Send this message whether or not the check-in is held. The presentation work in the coming weeks cannot proceed without format, timing, and attendees confirmed with the employer.

**What to confirm:**

- **Presentation format:** live in person, live remote, recorded, or hybrid
- **Date and time:** confirmed date and start time, including time zone
- **Duration:** how long the presentation is expected to run, including time for Q&A
- **Attendees:** who from the employer organization will be present; any additional stakeholders who will attend

**Tone:** Administrative and forward-looking. This is a logistics message, not a check-in. If the check-in was cancelled, a brief note confirming the team is on track is appropriate. It signals that the cancellation was deliberate, not an oversight.

**Template:**

> **Subject:** Final Presentation logistics — [Team/Project name]
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> We're on track for submitting our Final Deliverables this week and wanted to confirm a few logistics for the Final Presentation. Could you let us know the preferred format (in person, remote, or hybrid), the confirmed date and time, expected duration, and who from your team will be attending?
>
> We're looking forward to sharing the finished work. Please let us know if there's anything you need from us before then.
>
> [Team sign-off]
