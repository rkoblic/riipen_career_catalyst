# What's next

This week's Near-Final Check-In is the last major employer input before the final submission. The coming weeks translate that input into the final version. If the employer's feedback is thin, which can happen if you've been in close contact to date, next week covers how to triage what you do have and use an AI quality reviewer as a second input.

---

## The final deliverable submission

The next major milestone is the Final Deliverables submission, graded against the rubric you've been using throughout the Refinement phase. Enter the final development push with a clear view of the rubric dimensions and a revision list from this week's debrief. Teams that track the arc as they go (here's what we heard, here's what we decided, here's where it shows up) produce stronger final submissions and complete the Responsiveness to Feedback section of the Change Log more easily.

Your next standing check-in is the week of the final submission. By then your team should be executing on its revision list rather than holding open questions, so bring any final ones if you have them. Otherwise, requesting to skip is fine.

---

## Reflection #4 is coming

Reflection #4 is the next individual graded submission. By the time you draft it, your team will be close enough to a finished deliverable to see the distance between where the project started and where it ended up.

**The prompt:** Choose a specific moment from the past three weeks where your understanding of the project, the deliverable, or your own capabilities shifted visibly from what you assumed earlier in the course. Use the DEAL framework to describe what happened, examine what caused the shift, and articulate what you now understand about connecting prior knowledge to real work that you didn't understand when the project began.

Start noticing now. The moments that best anchor this reflection are small realizations during the work that the deliverable is different from what the original plan described, or a piece of feedback that landed differently than earlier feedback did. Catching those while the project is still live gives you more to draw on when you draft.

---

## How to prepare

- **Assign and track the revisions from this week's Near-Final Check-In.** Each piece needs a name, an intended change, and a target for completion. Update the Change Log as revisions are made, not after the fact.
- **Decide whether your team needs the next standing check-in.** If you have a final question, bring it; otherwise, requesting to skip is fine.
- **Start noticing candidate moments for Reflection #4.** A short note in your team's working notes is enough.
- **Look ahead to the presentation phase**, which opens next week. The outline gets drafted as part of that work; nothing to prepare in advance.
