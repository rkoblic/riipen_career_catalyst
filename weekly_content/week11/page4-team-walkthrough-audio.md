# Audio Script: A team walkthrough in practice

**Scene:** The near-final review meeting. A three-person student team (Maya, James, Priya) is meeting with their employer, Dana, a volunteer coordinator at a community nonprofit. The team has been working on a volunteer retention strategy. Dana has skimmed the package but has not read it in depth.

**Length:** ~3.5 minutes at typical pace. Four speakers.

---

**Dana:** Hi everyone. I got the package Monday. Honestly, I've only had time to skim it. Did want to make sure we used the time well today.

**Maya:** Totally. That's what we were expecting to hear, so we've planned around it. We'll walk you through the shape of the package, hit three things we specifically want your read on, and leave time at the end for anything you want to raise. Sound good?

**Dana:** Sounds great.

**Maya:** So the package is structured in four parts. Section one is the situational analysis, which covers what we found about current volunteer retention patterns at Harbor Street. Section two is the three priority recommendations we're proposing. Section three is a 90-day implementation roadmap tied to each recommendation. And section four is a set of measurement approaches so you can tell whether the changes are working.

The Change Log is attached as a separate document. It tracks every piece of feedback we've gotten from you across our check-ins and what we did with it.

James, you want to take the Change Log?

**James:** Yeah. The main thing to flag. At the check-in two weeks ago, you pushed back on our original recommendation that centered recruitment. You said the bigger issue is that volunteers who do sign up don't come back for a second shift, and we should be more focused on retention. We agreed, and you'll see that shift reflected in section two. The retention recommendation is now the lead, and the recruitment piece moved down to a supporting role.

There's also a note in the Change Log about your suggestion to include cost estimates for each recommendation. We included rough ranges where we could. For two of the three recommendations, we didn't have enough information to estimate credibly, so we flagged those as "to be scoped with your finance lead" rather than putting numbers we weren't confident in.

**Dana:** Appreciate that. The retention pivot is the right move.

**Maya:** Good. Priya, you want to take the first question?

**Priya:** Yeah. So our first open question is about section three. We built the 90-day roadmap on the assumption that the volunteer coordinator role stays at half-time through Q3. If that role gets expanded to full-time earlier, some of the sequencing we've proposed would change. The onboarding redesign in particular would land better in month two rather than month three. Is there anything you can share about how that role might evolve?

**Dana:** That's a fair thing to raise. We've had the funding conversation twice this quarter. The short answer is we'd love to expand it but probably can't until fall. So your assumption is correct for the 90-day window, but I'd flag it in the roadmap as something that could shift, rather than treating it as locked in.

**Priya:** That's helpful. We can add that as an explicit assumption in the roadmap footer.

**Dana:** Good. Can I jump in with something I noticed while skimming?

**Maya:** Please do.

**Dana:** The measurement section, section four, feels lighter than the other sections. I don't know if that's a scoping choice or if you ran short on it.

**James:** Partly both, honestly. We scoped it to three measurement areas rather than going wider because we thought anything more would get skipped. But we can add detail on what specifically to track in each area if that's more useful.

**Dana:** That would help. Especially if you can name the quarterly numbers I should be watching.

**James:** Got it. That'll go on the revision list before the final submission.

**Maya:** Good transition. We have two more targeted questions, then we'll leave the last five minutes for anything else on your side. Next question is about section two.

---

## Production notes

- Four voices needed (three team members + employer). Distinct-sounding voices matter more than distinct accents; the listener needs to track handoffs by ear.
- Natural pacing, not performed. Short pauses between speakers. Avoid overly polished delivery — this should sound like real people talking, not a radio drama.
- "Honestly, I've only had time to skim it" and "Partly both, honestly" are the key naturalism anchors. Don't smooth those out in direction.
- The Harbor Street reference echoes Week 10's example; preserve the name if consistency across weeks matters.
