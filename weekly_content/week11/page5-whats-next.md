# What's next

The Near-Final Review feedback is the last major input before the final submission. The coming sessions are where that feedback gets translated into the final version of your deliverable.

---

## The final deliverable submission

The next major milestone is the Final Deliverables submission, which is graded. The rubric you've been referencing throughout the Refinement phase is the same rubric used to grade the final submission. Your team should enter the final production push with a clear view of the rubric dimensions and a specific list of what still needs to change, drawn from the Near-Final Review debrief.

The Near-Final Review feedback, the Change Log you've been building since the Direction Check, and the revision priority list from your debrief are the inputs. The final deliverable is the output. Teams that track this arc explicitly (here's what we heard, here's what we decided, here's where it shows up) produce stronger final submissions and have a much easier time completing the Responsiveness to Feedback section of the Change Log.

There is also an optional Final Check-In available before the final submission. Whether to hold it depends on whether your team has substantive questions that would change the final deliverable. If you do, schedule it and bring those questions. If the Near-Final Review answered what you needed to know, canceling the optional meeting is a professional judgment, not a shortcut.

---

## Reflection #4

The next individual graded submission is Reflection #4. The prompt asks you to examine the gap between the plan your team made at the start of the project and the work your team actually produced.

If you read the Reflection #4 prompt when it was previewed earlier, you've already been noticing. If you haven't read it yet, now is the right time. The gap between plan and product is clearest while the project is still live.

---

## How to prepare

- **Assign and track the Near-Final Review revisions.** Each piece of feedback from the review should have a name, a description of the intended change, and a target for completion. Update the Change Log as revisions are made, not after the fact.
- **Read the Final Deliverables rubric** if you haven't already. Your team's revision priorities should map directly to the rubric dimensions.
- **Decide whether to hold the optional Final Check-In.** If you have substantive questions that would change the final deliverable, schedule it. If not, use the time for production.
- **Begin planning the Final Presentation.** The presentation phase opens soon. Preparation that starts early, including outlining the narrative and assigning roles, tends to produce clearer presentations than preparation that starts close to the deadline.
