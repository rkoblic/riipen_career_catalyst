# What's next

This week's meeting is the last major employer input before the final submission. The coming sessions translate that input into the final version. If the employer's feedback is thin, which happens when they skim, next week covers how to triage what you have and use the AI quality reviewer as a second input.

---

## The final deliverable submission

The next major milestone is the Final Deliverables submission, which is graded against the rubric you've been using throughout the Refinement phase. Enter the final production push with a clear view of the rubric dimensions and a specific revision list drawn from this week's debrief. Teams that track the arc explicitly (here's what we heard, here's what we decided, here's where it shows up) produce stronger final submissions and have a much easier time completing the Responsiveness to Feedback section of the Change Log.

Your next standing check-in falls in the week before the final submission. Bring substantive questions if you have them. If this week's review answered what you needed to know, requesting to skip is a professional judgment rather than a shortcut.

---

## Reflection #4 is coming

Reflection #4 is the next individual graded submission. By the time you draft it, your team will be close enough to a finished deliverable to see the distance between where the project started and where it ended up.

**The prompt:** Choose a specific moment from the past three weeks where your understanding of the project, the deliverable, or your own capabilities shifted visibly from what you assumed earlier in the course. Use the DEAL framework to describe what happened, examine what caused the shift, and articulate what you now understand about connecting prior knowledge to real work that you didn't understand when the project began.

Start noticing now. The moments that best anchor this reflection are small realizations during production that the work is different from what the original plan described, or a piece of feedback that landed differently than earlier feedback did. Catching those while the project is still live gives you more to draw on when you draft.

---

## How to prepare

- **Assign and track the revisions from this week's review.** Each piece needs a name, an intended change, and a target for completion. Update the Change Log as revisions are made, not after the fact.
- **Think about what you'd use your next standing check-in for.** If you have questions that would change the final deliverable, bring them. If not, requesting to skip is fine.
- **Start noticing candidate moments for Reflection #4.** A short note in your team's working notes is enough.
- **Look ahead to the presentation phase**, which opens next week. The outline gets drafted as part of that work; nothing to prepare in advance.
