# Video Script: What document reviews actually test

Professional documents get reviewed in two very different ways: someone reads them independently and forms their own assessment, or someone walks through them with the author in the room. Work produced under time pressure often gets optimized for the second scenario. The author plans to be there to explain the gaps.

The Near-Final Review is a document review, which means your employer reads the package before the meeting. They come in with their own read, their own list of what's complete, what's unclear, and what they can't follow without someone filling in the context. They're not reacting to questions you've prepared. They're presenting their own assessment.

That changes what preparation looks like. In a working session, you bring specific questions and ask the employer to respond. In a document review, the document has already answered, or failed to answer, what the employer needed to know. Your job in the meeting is to walk through what you've incorporated from prior feedback, and to be precise about what's still unresolved.

What you submit is what the employer reads.
