# Quality and craft

Before your employer reads the near-final package, your team should read it. Not to revise content, but to evaluate it. This module covers what professional quality looks like at this stage and how to run the internal review gate that should happen before the employer sees the work.

---

## What quality means at this stage

When a document gets reviewed independently (by an employer before a meeting, by a client before a presentation), the things that surface first are rarely about the quality of the analysis. They're about the consistency and coherence of the package as a whole.

These are assembly problems, not writing problems. They arrive when sections built separately by different team members get placed next to each other for the first time. Each section was fine on its own. Together, the seams show.

The difference between complete and polished is often not content. It's consistency. A deliverable can be analytically strong and substantively right, but still communicate that unevenly to a reader who doesn't know the team that built it.

**Connection to the Final Deliverables rubric**

The Deliverable Quality dimension of the Final Deliverables rubric is the formal measure of this. It covers formatting consistency, visual coherence, and whether the document reads as a unified, professionally presented whole. The Near-Final Review is evaluated against the same standard as the final submission. Knowing what the rubric asks for before you submit the near-final package lets your team prioritize what to address in the polishing pass.

## Self-review before client review

The self-review is the team's last internal pass before the employer reads the work. It runs differently from a normal reading. You're reading to evaluate, not to understand. The video below demonstrates the specific categories of error that surface in assembled documents.

### Self-review checklist

- [ ] **Terminology is consistent throughout:** the same concept uses the same word every time it appears in the document
- [ ] **All cross-references are accurate:** any phrase like "as discussed above" or "see the next section" still points to something that exists in the current version
- [ ] **Formatting is consistent for similar content:** recommendations, examples, and data points receive the same visual treatment across sections
- [ ] **Visual elements align with the text:** any chart, table, or figure is referenced in the body text and accurately reflects what the surrounding text describes
- [ ] **Recommendations are findable:** any recommendation included in the body also appears in the summary or implementation section, not only in one location
- [ ] **Prose is error-free:** spelling, punctuation, and grammar have been checked in each section, not only in sections that were recently revised

### Using AI to supplement the self-review

The AI quality reviewer approach you practiced recently extends directly to this moment. Run your assembled sections through the fresh reader and consistency check prompts before you start the manual checklist pass. What the model flags becomes input to your review. Items that AI notices and your team also notices independently are real issues. Items that AI flags but your team doesn't see may be noise, or may be worth a closer look.

One prompt that's useful at this stage: ask the model to identify the three weakest sections and explain specifically why each is weak. This surfaces whether the analytical weight of the document is evenly distributed, which is harder to see from inside the document than it is for an unfamiliar reader. A package where early sections are strong and later sections coast is a pattern the model tends to name.

AI can catch surface inconsistencies and flag structural gaps. It cannot evaluate whether your recommendations are grounded in the research you conducted or whether your analysis reflects what the employer actually needs. Those judgments are yours. The defend-your-work standard applies at the Near-Final Review, and the employer may ask your team to walk through specific sections or explain specific choices.
