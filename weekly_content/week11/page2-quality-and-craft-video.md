# Video Script: How errors hide in plain sight

After weeks of working on a document, you can't read it the way an unfamiliar reader will. You know what you meant. You know what the missing context is. You know which assumption bridges two paragraphs that don't quite connect on the page. The reader doesn't.

That gap shows up in predictable places.

The first is a missing recommendation. A market entry strategy has a strong analysis section that identifies three underserved customer segments. The recommendations section addresses two of them. The third gets mentioned in the body but never recommended on. The team knows what happened. That section was drafted last, time ran short, and the gap got noted as something to return to. It never got returned to. A reader coming to the document fresh has no way of knowing that. They just see a finding with no follow-through.

The second is orphaned references. An employee onboarding guide that says "as outlined in the previous section" when that section was cut two weeks ago. The reference makes complete sense if you remember what used to be there. Otherwise, it's a sentence that points to nothing. These are among the hardest errors to catch yourself, because your memory fills in the gap automatically.

The third is formatting drift. An ESG strategy where the recommendations in section 2 are numbered steps, in section 4 are bullet points, and in section 5 are embedded in prose. Same type of content, three different treatments. Each section looked fine when it was drafted in isolation. Together, they read as if different teams wrote them.

None of these feel like errors when you're the author. Your eye skips them because your brain already knows what the document meant to say. The checklist on this page is designed to force you to look at each category directly, rather than reading through the document in the usual way.
