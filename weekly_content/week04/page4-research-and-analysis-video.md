# Video Script: From "what we found" to "what it means"

Say your team is working on a social media strategy for a small business. You've researched the competitive landscape, and you write something like this: "Competitor A has 12,000 Instagram followers. Competitor B has 8,500 followers and posts three times a week. Competitor C launched a TikTok account six months ago."

That's a summary. It's accurate, but it doesn't tell the reader anything about what it means.

Now here's the same research, synthesized: "All three direct competitors have shifted toward short-form video content in the past year, and the two that did it earliest have seen the strongest follower growth. This suggests the audience in this space responds to video, and our employer's current approach of static image posts may be falling behind a format shift that's already underway."

Same research. The second version identifies a pattern, draws a conclusion, and connects it to the project.

Here's another one. Your team is working on a community outreach program for a nonprofit, and you've researched participation barriers. As a summary, it might read: "A 2023 study found that 40% of youth in the target area don't participate in after-school programs. The organization currently promotes programs through school flyers and their website."

Synthesized, it goes deeper: "Published research consistently identifies transportation and scheduling conflicts, not awareness, as the primary barriers to youth program participation in communities like this one. The organization's current outreach relies on flyers and their website, which assume the problem is that families don't know about the programs. But if the real barrier is getting there, increasing awareness won't move the needle. This suggests the project should focus on accessibility, not marketing."

That version does something the first one didn't. It names a gap between what the research says and what the organization is currently doing, and it draws a conclusion about what that means for the project direction.

That shift, from reporting what you found to explaining what it means, is the difference between a data dump and analysis the employer can actually use.
