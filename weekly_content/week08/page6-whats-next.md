# What's next

You've completed the first peer evaluation and made the first structured pass at incorporating employer feedback. The production pressure continues — next week carries another large project work block and the second employer meeting of the Development phase.

---

## Check-In #2

The second employer progress meeting follows the same structure as Check-In #1: your team brings specific questions and decision points, the employer helps you move forward. The difference is that you're further into the work now. The questions you can ask are more specific, and the employer's input can be more targeted.

Start identifying your questions before the week ends. The sections you've been revising this week are a natural source — anywhere your team made a judgment call about how to address the employer's feedback, or anywhere the revision raised a new question, is a candidate for Check-In #2.

---

## Team Processing Session and peer evaluation results

Before Check-In #2, your peer evaluation results come back. You'll receive anonymized qualitative feedback from your teammates and your aggregated BARS averages by dimension. A structured Team Processing Session gives your team space to discuss the feedback and agree on specific changes to how you're working together.

To prepare, keep the evidence that informed your own evaluation close. When you see your results, you'll want to be able to think clearly about whether the ratings you received match your own sense of how you contributed — and where the gaps are worth examining.

---

## How to prepare

- **Keep advancing your draft.** The more complete your sections are before Check-In #2, the more specific your questions can be. Vague drafts produce vague questions.
- **Review your debrief notes from Check-In #1.** Any feedback item your team noted but set aside might look different after a week of production. Some notes-category feedback becomes act-on once you're further into the work.
- **Check the Check-In #2 materials** before the week starts so you know what preparation looks like. The structure is the same, but your questions should reflect where the project actually is.
