# Video Script: Why iteration is part of the work

A process improvement team sits down after Check-In #1 with five pieces of feedback in their notes. Here's how the sorting conversation goes.

First one: "Your current-state workflow diagram doesn't account for what happens when a ticket gets escalated." That changes the analysis. If the escalation path is missing, the recommendations built on top of it may be incomplete. Act on.

Second: "Our IT team is planning a system migration in Q3 — it's not finalized, but it might affect the workflow." Useful context. Doesn't require a change now, but belongs in the project record in case the recommendations include system-dependent steps. Note it.

Third: "I personally find color-coded tables easier to read than plain ones." Style preference, not direction. The deliverable doesn't require color-coded tables and the project brief doesn't specify it. Set aside.

Fourth: "The section on response time benchmarks — I wasn't sure where those numbers came from." A citation is missing or the methodology needs to be explained. Targeted revision. Act on.

Fifth: "You might want to consider a section on staff training, depending on how the recommendations land." Tentative, conditional, not yet a direction. Note it — if the recommendations do require training steps, this becomes relevant later.

Five pieces of feedback. Two to act on, two to note, one to set aside. That conversation takes fifteen minutes. What it produces determines what the next week of production looks like.
