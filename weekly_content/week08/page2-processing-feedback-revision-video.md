# Video Script: Why iteration is part of the work

A revision cycle isn't a sign that the first draft failed. It's a sign that you've gotten information you didn't have before.

Think about what happened at Check-In #1. Your team went in with a draft and came out with the employer's perspective on it. That's exactly what the meeting was for. What you do with that perspective is where the real production work begins.

Here's the challenge most teams face: the instinct is to treat every piece of feedback as equally urgent and start revising everything at once. That's how teams lose track of what actually needs to change and end up spending time on sections that were already working.

A community outreach team gets feedback that their target population analysis is too narrow. That's a structural change, and it affects everything built on top of it. If the same team also hears that the employer prefers a different font in the tables, that's a preference, not a priority. Treating both with the same urgency is how the week falls apart.

Before you revise anything, sort what you heard. What needs to change? What's useful context? What can you consciously set aside? That sorting conversation, done as a team in the first session of the week, is what makes the rest of the production time efficient.
