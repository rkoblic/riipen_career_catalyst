# Video Script: Why iteration is part of the work

Your team went into Check-In #1 with a draft and came out with the employer's perspective on it. That's exactly what the meeting was for. What you do with that perspective is where the real production work begins.

The instinct is to treat every piece of feedback as equally urgent and start revising everything at once. That's how teams lose track of what actually needs to change and end up spending time on sections that were already working.

Take a community outreach team that hears two things from the employer: "Your target population analysis is focused on the wrong age group" and "I personally prefer wider margins in reports." The first one changes the scope of a section and everything that follows from it. The second is a style preference. If the team spends Monday redesigning the document format and gets to the population analysis on Thursday, they've misread the week.

A social media strategy team might face the same situation differently. They hear "your competitive landscape missed our main actual competitor" and "the chart colors are a bit hard to read." Structural issue and a minor fix. Same sorting problem.

Before you revise anything, have the sorting conversation as a team. What needs to change, what's worth noting for later, and what can you consciously set aside. That conversation, done first, is what makes the rest of the production time productive.
