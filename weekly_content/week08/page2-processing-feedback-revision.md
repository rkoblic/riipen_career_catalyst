# Processing feedback and revision

The video above walks through a team sorting five pieces of real feedback in real time. Use the reference below while your team does the same with your own debrief notes.

[VIDEO: Why iteration is part of the work]

---

## The three categories

| Category | What it is | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Act on** | Feedback that changes what you produce — scope corrections, missing analysis, clear direction changes | Assign an owner and a deadline before your team disperses |
| **Note** | Context that informs but doesn't change the current work — organizational background, upcoming changes, validation of your approach | Log it in your project record; it may become relevant in the Change Log or later sections |
| **Set aside** | Scope-adjacent preferences, tentative suggestions, or comments that conflict with the project brief | Document the decision to set it aside so it's visible to the team |

---

## Quick examples

*Act on — a community outreach team hears: "Your target population section focuses on adults 25–45, but we've actually been trying to reach older residents. The 25–45 group is secondary." Scope change. Act on before drafting continues.*

*Note — a process improvement team hears: "We're piloting a new ticketing system in one department — not rolled out yet, but it might change the workflow in six months." Useful context for recommendations. Note it.*

*Set aside — a social media strategy team hears: "Personally, I've always liked the idea of a weekly newsletter, but that's just me." Preference outside the project brief. Set aside.*

## Building a revision plan

Once sorted, convert your act-on items into a concrete plan.

[TEMPLATE: Feedback Response Plan — columns: feedback item | category | revision required | owner | target date]

**Assign one owner per item.** "The whole team" owns nothing.

**Distinguish revision type before estimating time.** A targeted edit takes an hour; a structural revision takes a day. The next page covers this distinction.

**Keep it in a shared document and add to it throughout the project.** This plan is the seed of your Change Log — a running record submitted with your final deliverable that documents the feedback your team received and how you addressed it. Every time you incorporate employer input, the Change Log grows. Start it now.
