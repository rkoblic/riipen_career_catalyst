# Video Script: From feedback to forward motion

In most corporate environments, performance reviews are a standing rhythm of professional life. Once a year, sometimes twice, you get the chance to hear how others see your contribution to the team and the wider business: what's working, and where there's room to grow. Increasingly, that picture comes from more than just your manager. The peers and colleagues you work with day to day are part of it too.

It's easy to walk into these conversations expecting to be judged. But the people who get the most out of their careers tend to treat them as an opportunity. Honest feedback, delivered well, is one of the most direct routes to getting better at what you do, and to understanding how your work is actually landing with the people around you.

This week, you're on both sides of that exchange. You'll evaluate each of your teammates, and they'll evaluate you. Your employer is also sharing their read on how the team has been working with them. Next week, the peer feedback comes back to you individually. Your team will discuss the employer's feedback together.

The peer evaluation is going to take real time and care. Plan for that. What makes one useful is the specific behavior behind each rating. Vague praise and vague criticism both leave the reader without anything to act on. Treat others how you hope to be treated when it's your turn. If leading a team is somewhere in your future, this is the kind of skill worth practicing early.
