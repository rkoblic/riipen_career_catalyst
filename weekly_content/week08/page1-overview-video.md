# Video Script: From feedback to forward motion

In most corporate environments, performance reviews are a standing rhythm of professional life. Once a year, sometimes twice, you sit through a review of your work. And increasingly, those reviews include a 360-degree element. Feedback comes not just from your manager, but from your peers and the people you work with day to day.

This week is built around the same pattern. You'll evaluate each of your teammates on their contribution to the project so far, and each of them will evaluate you. Your employer is also giving their read on how the team has been working with them. Next week, the results come back for your team to discuss together.

The part that takes most of your time and judgment is writing the peer evaluation. What makes a peer evaluation useful is the specific behavior behind each rating. That's the skill any manager has to develop to write a performance review worth reading. If you think leading a team is somewhere in your future, this is the kind of skill worth practicing early.
