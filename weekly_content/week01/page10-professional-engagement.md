# How Professional Engagement Is Assessed

Most graded components in this course assess an artifact you produced — a research document, a presentation, a reflection. Professional Engagement is different. It captures *how you worked* across the entire semester: whether you showed up reliably for your team, communicated clearly, contributed substantively, and handled feedback and conflict with maturity. The other deliverables show what you produced. Professional Engagement shows how you worked alongside the people who depended on you.

The reason this exists in your grade is the same reason professional roles include performance reviews and 360-feedback in addition to project outcomes. In professional practice, your reputation is built less from any single deliverable than from the pattern of how you show up across many of them.

> [!key]
> **Professional Engagement is not a single grade.** It is the unifying frame for **five separate graded items** that together total **20% of your final grade**. You will see each one as its own entry in your gradebook.

---

## The five graded items

| Graded item | Source | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| [**GRADED: Mid-Project Peer Evaluation**](https://riipen.instructure.com/courses/107/pages/graded-mid-project-peer-evaluation) (Week 8) | Submitted by your teammates | 3% |
| [**GRADED: End-of-Project Peer Evaluation**](https://riipen.instructure.com/courses/107/pages/graded-end-of-project-peer-evaluation) (Week 15) | Submitted by your teammates | 7% |
| [**GRADED: Mid-Project Employer Feedback**](https://riipen.instructure.com/courses/107/pages/about-the-employer-mid-project-evaluation) (around Week 8) | Submitted by your employer | 1.5% |
| [**GRADED: End-of-Project Employer Feedback**](https://riipen.instructure.com/courses/107/pages/about-the-employer-end-of-project-evaluation) (after the Final Presentation) | Submitted by your employer | 3.5% |
| [**GRADED: Instructor Observation**](https://riipen.instructure.com/courses/107/pages/graded-instructor-observation) (end of semester) | Assigned by your instructor | 5% |
| **Total** |  | **20%** |

Peer evaluations carry the largest combined weight because your teammates see parts of your contribution no one else does. The instructor doesn't sit in on your team's working sessions; the employer sees the team as a unit, not who actually did what. Peers know who did the work, who followed through, and who collaborated. The other two sources round out the picture: the employer's view of how the team handles itself professionally, and the instructor's view of patterns visible from outside the team.

The end-of-project peer evaluation and end-of-project employer feedback are weighted more heavily than their mid-project counterparts, on the logic that the longer arc of working together produces more reliable observations than the first half does on its own.

## About peer evaluations

You complete two structured peer evaluations during the course:

- **Mid-Project Peer Evaluation (Week 8)** — formative. The feedback you receive from your teammates is yours alone. It's not a group discussion item, and it's anonymized.
- **End-of-Project Peer Evaluation (Week 15)** — summative. The same instrument, calibrated across the full project arc.

Each evaluation rates your teammates across four dimensions: **Reliability**, **Contribution**, **Constructive Engagement**, and **Accountability**. The full instrument is explained on the linked pages.

> [!info] A note on honesty
> Peer evaluations only work as a fair grade component if people complete them honestly, including the hard parts. Inflating ratings to be nice doesn't help the teammate it's about — they don't learn anything from a generic "great teammate" rating — and it weakens the grading mechanism for everyone. The course design assumes you can write specific, professional feedback even when it's uncomfortable. That assumption is itself a skill you're building.

## About employer feedback

Your employer completes two short surveys during the project:

- **Mid-Project Employer Feedback (around Week 8).** Three rating dimensions — **Communication**, **Preparedness**, **Responsiveness** — plus qualitative feedback.
- **End-of-Project Employer Feedback (after the Final Presentation).** The same dimensions, plus an overall partnership-quality signal.

You will not see the employer's ratings directly. Employer feedback is a **team-level** input — the employer sees the team as a unit and rates it accordingly, so every team member starts from the same employer score. Your instructor may adjust an individual team member's employer-component score up or down by a modest amount when peer evaluations make clear that one teammate's contribution diverged substantially from the team's overall pattern. The default is that everyone on the team gets the same employer-component score.

What the employer is rating is straightforward: whether your team is easy to work with. Did you respond to messages in a reasonable time? Did you arrive at meetings prepared? Was the communication clear? These are the same things any client notices in any professional engagement.

## About instructor observation

This is a single end-of-semester rating your instructor assigns based on what they observed across the course. Unlike peer and employer feedback, which use structured rating instruments, instructor observation is a holistic 1–4 score informed by patterns visible across the full 15 weeks. Four kinds of evidence inform it:

- **Checkpoint patterns.** Were team checkpoints (Kickoff Summary, Direction Check Summary, Work-in-Progress Summary, Draft Deliverable) submitted on time, with genuine team effort?
- **Communication quality.** Were your emails and questions clear, professional, and timely? Did you reach out proactively when something needed clarification?
- **Feedback responsiveness.** When your instructor gave formative feedback on checkpoints or graded work, did the next iteration show evidence the feedback had been taken on?
- **Professional judgment.** How did your team handle conflict, missed expectations, or moments of pressure? Did the team communicate early when something was off-track?

The instructor's role is intentionally different from the other two sources. Peers see the inside of the team. Employers see the outside. The instructor sees the seams: the moments when something starts to wobble, the responses to feedback, the patterns over time that neither peers nor the employer have a frame for.

## What this means for your week-to-week work

There's no single moment you can prepare for Professional Engagement. You can't write a strong submission the night before it's due, because there isn't one. The five items above are produced by patterns: what your teammates have come to expect from you, what your employer has seen across multiple touchpoints, what your instructor has observed in checkpoints and communications across 15 weeks.

The practical implication is short:

- **Be reliable in small things.** Show up to meetings, respond to messages, follow through on what you said you'd do. Most of what builds strong scores here is the unglamorous stuff.
- **Communicate early when something's off** — a deadline you can't meet, a piece of feedback you don't understand, a tension on the team. Proactive communication is what your instructor and employer are watching for.
- **Take feedback seriously.** When you receive formative feedback (from peers, from employer touchpoints, from your instructor's comments on checkpoints), the next version of your work should show evidence you engaged with it.
- **Write peer evaluations honestly.** The system depends on it, and the skill of writing specific, evidence-based feedback to a colleague is part of what the course is teaching you to do.
