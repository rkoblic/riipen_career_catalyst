# Video Script: What transferable learning actually sounds like

The gap between what you did and what you learned is easier to see in concrete examples. Here's what it looks like across three different kinds of project.

Start with a community outreach project for a nonprofit. A learner might write, "I learned how the Harbor Street Center runs their volunteer orientations." That's a sentence about the work, not about what they learned. The learning version sounds like this: "I learned that volunteer orientation works best when it front-loads what the volunteer will actually do, rather than the organization's history. People retain more when they can picture the task they'll do next week." The same gap shows up in a process improvement project.

A learner might write, "I learned about the invoice approval workflow at this company." That describes the work, not the takeaway. The learning version sounds more like this: "I learned that written workflows rarely match the actual workflows. The official version is the starting point. The useful version comes from asking people what they do when the official steps don't work." Take a learner working on social media strategy for a small business.

They might write, "I learned what kind of content performs best on their Instagram." That's a description of what was studied, not what was learned. The learning version might sound like this: "I learned how to tell when a content strategy is sustainable. A pattern can work for six weeks and then stop because the person running it runs out of variations. The strategy itself was fine." Each strong version still sounds specific.

But every one of them could also apply in different projects and scenarios. That's what makes them transferable.
