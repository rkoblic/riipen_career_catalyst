# Video script: How to walk through a document with a stakeholder

You have 30 minutes. Going through every part of the document in order won't leave you time for the parts where you actually need input.

Open by framing the conversation. Tell the employer you'd like to highlight a few key areas and then get their feedback, rather than walk through the whole document. That signals you're going to use the time well.

For those highlights, choose the areas where the employer's perspective matters most. That usually means your strongest finding, the recommendation you're most confident about, and somewhere you'd like their input because you're not sure yet.

Here's what that sounds like. Say your team is working on a community outreach project for a nonprofit. Your first highlight might go like this: "The most important thing our research found is that the primary barrier to participation in programs like yours isn't awareness. It's transportation and scheduling. Three published studies on similar communities found the same pattern, and the organization's own data shows that sign-ups are highest in the neighborhoods closest to the current location.

That's what's driving our recommendation." For your second highlight, walk through the recommendation itself: "Based on that finding, we're recommending a satellite-location pilot at two community centers that are accessible by public transit. We chose those locations because they're in the neighborhoods with the lowest current participation rates but the highest population density in the target age group." Lead with the reasoning. The employer can see what you chose and why, which makes it easy for them to redirect if needed.

For your third highlight, name where you're uncertain: "The assumption we're least sure about is whether the community centers have the space and willingness to host programming. We haven't been able to confirm that yet.If that assumption doesn't hold, we'd need to rethink the location strategy. That's something we'd especially like your perspective on." After your highlights, transition to feedback with a specific question, not a vague one.

A question that will get you a real answer might be something like: "Does the transportation barrier match what you're seeing, or is there something else driving the participation gap?" A question that might get you silence is more like: "Any questions?"
