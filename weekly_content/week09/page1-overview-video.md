# Video Script: What assembling a draft actually tests

There's a moment in most team projects when the work stops being a collection of parts and becomes a thing. Someone pastes the sections together and reads it through. That moment is either reassuring or uncomfortable, and it's almost never nothing.

What you often find is that the pieces are individually fine, but the whole reads like different people wrote it, because different people did. That's not a failure. It's a structural problem with a solution. But it only gets fixed if someone reads the full draft before it goes out.

The other thing that shows up at this stage is whether the work answers the question the employer actually needs answered. Not just "here's what we found" but "here's what you should do about it, and why." Teams that build strong research sometimes forget to close that gap.

This is also the week when feedback arrives from multiple directions at once: peer evaluation results, the employer's mid-project survey, and your employer in Check-In #2. The teams that handle this week well don't treat any of those as interruptions. They treat them as the best information they have access to right now, and they use it.
