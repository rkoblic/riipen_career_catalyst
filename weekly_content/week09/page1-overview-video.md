# Video Script: The work most professionals never get taught

When feedback about how you're working arrives, especially when it's surprising, the instinct is usually to explain yourself. The context, the reasons, why things looked the way they did. That instinct isn't wrong. It just tends to happen out loud, in front of the people who gave the feedback, before you've actually sat with what they said.

Most professionals never get taught how to do the part that comes first. That part is reading what landed on you, noticing your reaction, pushing past your first interpretation, and deciding what you actually want to do about it. It happens privately, if it happens at all, and it shapes everything that follows.

This week your team gets two kinds of feedback. The employer's survey on how the project is going is what your team processes together in the session. That's normal client work. The peer evaluation, the comments your teammates wrote about how you've been to work with, is yours alone to work through before the team meets. The conversation worth having with your team is about what you've decided to change, not about what your teammates wrote.

The question to take into that individual work is about yourself. When you read something that surprises you, are you there to process it, or to defend against it? The answer changes what you bring to the room.
