# Video Script: What assembling a draft actually tests

The most useful feedback in any project comes when there's still time to act on it. Once work is finalized, feedback gets softer — there's nothing left to change. The draft review is where you get the employer's honest read while you can still do something with it.

There's also a shift in what your employer is assessing. At earlier check-ins, they were validating direction: are you focused on the right problem, is the approach reasonable. Now they're assessing quality: is this good enough to actually be useful? Those are different questions, and teams that prepare for Check-In #2 the same way they prepared for Check-In #1 tend to get less out of it.

The teams that get the most from a draft review come in with specific questions about the quality of their work, not just its progress. "Is the depth of this analysis sufficient for what you need?" is a question an employer can actually answer. "Are we on track?" is one they've already moved past.

That's what this week is designed to test: whether your team can shift from building to evaluating, and use what you learn while there's still time to improve the work.
