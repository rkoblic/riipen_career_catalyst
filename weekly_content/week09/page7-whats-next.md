# What's next

You've submitted your draft and heard directly from the employer about where the work stands. The project now enters its final production push toward the near-final package.

---

## The near-final package

The next major milestone is a near-final version of your deliverable, submitted for a document review meeting with the employer. This is different from Check-In #2, where the employer responded to questions you brought. In the document review, the deliverable speaks for itself. The employer reads the near-final package in advance and comes with their own assessment of what's complete, what's missing, and what needs strengthening before the final version.

The weeks between now and then are the time to act on everything you've learned: the feedback from Check-In #2, the team processing agreement, the gaps identified in your internal review gate. Coming into the document review with work that hasn't substantively changed since the draft is a missed opportunity.

---

## The Change Log

Starting now, your team should be maintaining a Change Log: a running document that records the feedback you've received, the decisions you made in response, and how those decisions show up in the deliverable. When you submit the near-final package, the Change Log goes with it.

The Change Log matters because it shows the employer that your team didn't just receive their feedback — you engaged with it. A document review where the team can say "here's what you raised at Check-In #1, here's what you raised at Check-In #2, and here's how we addressed each one" is a substantially different experience than one where the employer has to wonder whether their input had any effect.

Your debrief notes from Check-In #2 are the starting point. Log what the employer said, what you decided to do about it, and what changed in the document as a result.

---

## How to prepare

- **Act on Check-In #2 feedback promptly.** The feedback is freshest now. The longer you wait to address specific items, the more time you'll spend reconstructing what was meant.
- **Assign ownership for each revision.** Every piece of feedback from Check-In #2 should have a name and a target date attached to it before your team disperses. A shared action item is an unowned one.
- **Keep the Change Log current.** Updating it as you make revisions is much easier than reconstructing it from memory before the document review submission.
