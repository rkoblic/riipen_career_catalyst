# The Draft Deliverable

In professional project work, clients and sponsors typically see work-in-progress before they see a final product. Not because the in-progress version is ready, but because showing work early creates the conditions for feedback that can actually change the final product. A draft that goes out for review gives the employer something real to react to, which produces feedback that's more specific and more useful than feedback on an outline or a summary of what the team is planning to do.

The Draft Deliverable is your team's assembled current best effort: all major sections present, reflecting the revisions your team made after Check-In #1, and ready for substantive employer feedback in Check-In #2.

---

## What this checkpoint is (and isn't)

The Draft Deliverable is a completion-based team checkpoint. It is not graded for quality. What's assessed is whether your team has made substantive progress and produced genuine work product.

It is not a polished final document. Rough edges, incomplete sections, and areas you know need more work are all normal at this stage. What makes a draft different from a placeholder is that it reflects genuine thinking and real work, not a list of topics your team intends to address.

The team processing agreement produced in your Team Processing Session is appended to this submission. It is part of the checkpoint.

---

## What the draft should include

All major sections of your deliverable, in draft form. "Major sections" means the primary components defined in your project plan, not supplementary materials, appendices, or optional content.

For each section:

- The analysis or content your team has developed, in prose or structured format appropriate to your deliverable type
- The recommendations or outputs that follow from that analysis, even if they're still rough
- Source citations or references where relevant

Sections that aren't fully developed should still be present. A section that's in rough form is more useful to Check-In #2 than a gap with a note that it's coming. Include what you have.

---

## Completion criteria

A submission meets the standard if it:

- Contains all major sections in draft form, even if some are incomplete
- Reflects genuine work product (not outlines, bullet-point placeholders, or intent statements)
- Includes the team processing agreement as an appendix
- Includes an AI disclosure statement

A draft that has been structurally assembled but contains sections that are outlines or placeholders does not meet the criteria. If your team is genuinely behind on a section, submit what exists and name the gap clearly in the document rather than leaving it unmarked.

---

## AI guidance

The production-oriented AI skills introduced over the previous weeks apply here. Using AI for structural review of the assembled draft, stress-testing recommendations for actionability, checking for consistency across sections, and organizing your team's notes into a revision plan are all appropriate uses.

AI cannot produce the substance of the deliverable. The research, analysis, findings, and recommendations must reflect your team's genuine intellectual work. The test is the same as it has been throughout the course: could you explain and defend every claim in the document without referring back to what an AI produced? Check-In #2 is a live accountability mechanism where the employer will ask about the work you've built. Anything you can't speak to doesn't belong in the draft.

**AI disclosure:** Required on this submission. If AI was used at any point in drafting or organizing the deliverable, include a brief description of how. If AI was not used, include a one-sentence statement to that effect.

---

## Submission

**Draft Deliverable** — team submission, due before Check-In #2.

Submit the assembled draft with the team processing agreement appended. The submission is completion-based.
