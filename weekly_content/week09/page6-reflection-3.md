# Reflection #3

By this point in the course, the DEAL framework itself is familiar. The challenge in this third reflection is depth, not familiarity. The Describe and Experience stages of DEAL tend to come naturally once a learner has practiced them a few times. The Examine stage is where most reflections stop short, and where this one asks you to go further.

You have more to work with this time. The stretch of the course you're drawing from, the feedback and production period your team has been in, is the most concentrated of the whole project. There's more that happened, more decisions made under pressure, more moments where something worked or didn't. Depth comes from choosing the right moment and examining it closely, not from summarizing everything that's occurred.

---

## Going deeper in the Examine stage

The Examine stage asks why something happened and what it means, not just what happened. Most reflections that stall at the surface do so because the writer moves too quickly from describing the experience to stating a lesson. The lesson usually sounds true but feels generic ("I learned that communication is important") because it hasn't been tested against the specific details of what actually occurred.

Three moves that tend to deepen examination:

**Name the assumption behind the action.** What did you believe, expect, or take for granted that turned out to be incomplete or wrong? The interesting reflection usually starts here. "I assumed my teammate understood the scope the same way I did" opens an examination. "I learned to communicate more clearly" closes one prematurely.

**Identify the pattern, not just the instance.** Is this the first time you've encountered this kind of situation, or does it appear in your experience in other forms? A moment of conflict about credit, for example, may connect to other situations where you've had to navigate similar dynamics. The pattern is where professional self-knowledge lives.

**Name the professional implication.** Given what you observed and examined, what does it mean for how you'll handle similar situations in actual work contexts? This is different from stating a lesson. It's specific: "In a client-facing role where feedback is verbal and unstructured, I'll want to..." rather than "I'll be a better communicator."

---

## What counts as strong examination

The Reflection Portfolio rubric assesses the Examine stage for depth of insight, connection to professional contexts, and evidence of genuine self-assessment rather than performance of learning.

A weak Examine stage describes a feeling ("I felt frustrated when...") or restates the event with slightly more commentary. It doesn't investigate.

A strong Examine stage identifies something that was genuinely unclear or unknown to the writer before the reflection, explores why it happened rather than just that it happened, and connects the examination to something specific about how the writer understands professional work.

The difference is usually visible in the specificity of the language. "I realized teamwork requires clear expectations" is a restatement. "I realized I had been assuming that silence in our team channel meant agreement, when for two teammates it meant something different entirely" is an investigation.

## Reflection #3 assignment

**Focus:** A specific moment from the period your team has been in production, drawn from your experience of feedback or collaboration. You might draw from: the experience of receiving peer evaluation results, a moment in Check-In #1 or the preparation for it, the internal review gate, a conflict or tension in the team, or any other specific event from this period that taught you something about yourself or how you work.

Choose a moment with enough substance to examine closely, not the moment that was most dramatic or most comfortable.

**Structure:** Use the DEAL framework. The Describe and Experience stages should be concise. The Examine stage should be your longest and most developed section. The Apply/Learn stage should close with something specific and professionally grounded.

**Depth expectation:** This is your third DEAL reflection. The framework is no longer new, and the expectation for the Examine stage is higher than in earlier reflections. A reflection that applies the framework correctly but stays at the surface does not meet the standard for this submission.

**AI guidance:** The AI-as-Socratic-partner approach introduced at your first reflection applies here. Using AI to push back on your interpretation, to ask what else might be true about the experience, or to surface assumptions you haven't named yet is a genuinely useful way to deepen examination. Try a prompt like: "Here's my current interpretation of what happened and why: [paste your Examine draft]. Push back on this. What alternative explanations am I not considering? What assumptions am I making that I haven't questioned?"

AI cannot supply the experience, the examination, or the articulated learning. It can ask useful questions. The answers have to come from you.

**AI disclosure:** Required on this submission. If you used AI as a Socratic partner in developing your examination, note that in your disclosure. If you did not use AI, include a one-sentence statement to that effect.
