# What's next

You've submitted your draft and heard directly from the employer about where the work stands. The project now enters its final development push toward the near-final package.

---

## The near-final package

The next major milestone is a near-final version of your deliverable, due in two weeks, submitted for a document review meeting with the employer. This is different from the working-session check-in you just had, where the employer responded to questions you brought. In the document review, the deliverable speaks for itself. The employer reads the near-final package in advance and comes with their own assessment of what's complete, what's missing, and what needs strengthening before the final version.

The weeks between now and then are where the check-in feedback, the team processing agreement, and the gaps identified in your internal review gate should visibly reshape the draft. A strong document review is one where the employer can see that their input has already made it into the work, and your Change Log is what makes that visible. By the time of the review, your team should be able to walk through the near-final package and say "here's what you raised at our first check-in, here's what you raised at the second, and here's how we addressed each one."

---

## How to prepare

- **Act on check-in feedback promptly.** The feedback is freshest now. The longer you wait to address specific items, the more time you'll spend reconstructing what was meant.
- **Assign ownership for each revision.** Every piece of feedback from the check-in should have a name and a target date attached to it before your team disperses. Without that, shared ownership tends to mean nobody owns it.
- **Keep the Change Log current.** Updating it as you make revisions is much easier than reconstructing it from memory before the document review submission.
