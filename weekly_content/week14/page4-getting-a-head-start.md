# Getting a Head Start on Next Week

Next week, three pieces of work close out the course: the Final Presentation, Reflection #5, and the End-of-Project Peer Evaluation. The presentation is built and rehearsed this week. The other two are submitted individually next week, but both benefit from starting early. This page is about what is worth doing now so next week's work is finalization rather than construction under deadline pressure.

Both deliverables benefit from a few days between the early thinking and the final draft, and next week is already full with the Final Presentation. Use this page as the cue to start; the dedicated pages below carry the full prompts, rubric, and submission details.


---

## Reflection #5

Read the [Reflection #5 page](../week15/page4-reflection-5.md) for the full framing, including the video on selecting an anchor from across the arc. The goal for this sitting is to land on a candidate anchor and draft the Describe and Examine stages of the DEAL response. Articulate and Learn are the hardest stages of Reflection #5, and they benefit from the Final Presentation and team closing that will happen between this draft and the submission.

Aim to spend about an hour this week:

- **About twenty minutes on anchor selection.** Use the full-arc scan prompts on the Reflection #5 page. Do not commit to the first anchor that feels plausible. Consider two or three and pick the one with the strongest evidence across multiple weeks.
- **About thirty minutes drafting Describe and Examine.** Write the specific experience or pattern of experiences. Examine what produced the shift and what it reveals. If either section feels thin, that is a signal to reconsider the anchor, not to push through.
- **About ten minutes jotting notes toward Articulate and Learn.** First-pass candidate language only. The finished version comes together next week.

Do not try to finish the reflection in this block. Reflections written in one sitting consistently miss the Articulation of Learning bar.

## End-of-Project Peer Evaluation

Read the [End-of-Project Peer Evaluation page](../week15/page5-end-of-project-peer-evaluation.md) for the full framing, including how this evaluation differs from the Mid-Project version and how to evaluate contributions that evolved over time. You are setting yourself up for a strong submission next week, when the rehearsal and Final Presentation will add the last behavioral observations you need. The evaluation itself gets written after those.

Three steps you'll want to take this week:

- **Revisit the instructions.** Open the [Mid-Project Peer Evaluation page](../week08/page2-peer-evaluation-1.md) and the [End-of-Project Peer Evaluation page](../week15/page5-end-of-project-peer-evaluation.md). The Continue/Start/Adjust prompt language, the BARS scale descriptors across the four dimensions, and the specificity standard for evidence are the same. Familiarity with the framework is what keeps your evidence focused on behavior rather than impression.
- **Gather evidence across the full arc.** For each teammate, scan the project phase by phase and note specific behavioral examples. At this stage you are gathering observations, not assigning ratings. Aim for at least one piece of evidence per phase per teammate, and note where evidence is thin. Your Change Log, team meeting notes, and any notes-to-self you kept during earlier check-ins and working sessions are useful memory aids for the earlier phases. Reach for the specific, not the general. "She reliably brought drafts to the shared folder two days before each team sync" is evidence. "She was reliable" is not.
- **Build a short noticing list for the final week.** For each teammate, write down which dimensions have thin evidence and what you want to watch for during the rehearsal, Final Presentation, and team closing. Those events are the last sources of behavioral evidence you will have. Going into them with specific attention ("I need another observation on Constructive Engagement for teammate X") is more useful than trying to reconstruct observations after the fact.
