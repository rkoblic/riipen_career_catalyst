# Video Script: Saying I don't know well

The moment most teams worry about is the one where an employer asks a question you did not prepare for. It feels like the stakes of the whole presentation shift onto that single answer.

Here is what that moment usually sounds like when it goes poorly. An employer asks, "What would it take to scale this pilot to a second region?" A nervous team member says, "Oh, that's a really good question. I think we'd probably need to consider, you know, the different factors in that region, and maybe some additional resources, and then figure out how that connects to what we found." The words kept coming, but nothing in them was a real answer. The employer already knew the question was a good question. What they were listening for was what the team actually thought.

Now here is the same moment handled well. The team member pauses briefly and says, "We did not scope a second-region plan, so I can't give you a confident answer on what it would take. What I can tell you is that the finding that would most affect that decision is the one about user acquisition cost in your current market. If that cost doesn't hold in a new region, the economics change significantly. So the first thing I would want to know before scaling is whether we have any signal on acquisition in the new region."

Three things happened in that second answer. The speaker said plainly what they did not know, named the specific piece of the team's own work that is relevant to the question, and described what they would need to find out before answering confidently.

That is what defend-your-work looks like in a live Q&A. Not having every answer. Knowing what your own work means well enough to say what you would do next.
