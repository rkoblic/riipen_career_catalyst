# Video Script: The experience you can point to

Most recent graduates run into the same problem early in their careers. Employers want to see experience before they hire. Experience is hard to come by without a job. A long academic transcript does not answer the question a hiring manager is actually asking.

This project is an answer to that question. Fifteen weeks of work on a real project, for a real employer, that the employer will actually use. The story of that experience is what you will tell in job interviews, on a LinkedIn profile, in a graduate school application, in a conversation with a recruiter.

And the articulated version of that story is built now, while the work is still close. After six months of distance, the details blur. What you could have said with precision becomes something you can only sketch.

That is why this week matters. The reflection pulls fifteen weeks into something you can carry forward. The peer evaluation gives your teammates feedback they can use in their next team. The presentation shapes the deliverable into a story an employer can follow live, with you in the room.

A shipped project is only half of what this week is about. The other half is what you take with you, and that is shaped now, not later.
