# GRADED: End-of-Project Employer Feedback

After the Final Presentation, your employer is asked to complete an end-of-project evaluation of your team. The employer's ratings become this gradebook item — your team doesn't submit anything, but the score is one of the five graded items that together represent **Professional Engagement** in your final grade. See [**How Professional Engagement Is Assessed**](https://riipen.instructure.com/courses/107/pages/graded-professional-engagement) for the full picture. This page is what to know about it.

---

## What your employer is being asked

The end-of-project employer survey is short: seven items, about five minutes for the employer to complete. It is the summative version of the mid-project survey they completed back in Week 8.

The same three rated dimensions as mid-project, now framed across the full project:

- **Communication:** how clearly and professionally the team communicated through meetings, emails, and follow-ups across the entire engagement.
- **Preparedness:** how organized the team was for interactions, including clear agendas, materials ready, and thoughtful questions.
- **Responsiveness:** how the team followed up on input, asked clarifying questions, and stayed in touch between meetings.

Each is rated on the same four-level scale, with descriptions of what excellent, good, needs improvement, and unsatisfactory look like in practice.

The survey adds three end-of-project items:

- **Deliverable Quality.** The employer's rating of the final work product. This is the one dimension that could not be assessed mid-project, since the deliverables were not yet complete.
- **Partnership Quality.** A single overall question — "Would you work with this team again on a future project?" — with four response options from "definitely yes" to "definitely not." This is the strongest summary signal in the survey.
- An open-ended comment for the instructor, plus an overall professionalism rating that stays private.

---

## Why summative

The mid-project survey was formative. It caught your team's professionalism mid-way through the engagement, with time left to adjust. This end-of-project survey is summative. It captures the employer's full experience of the partnership, including the part of the project the mid-project survey did not cover.

The two surveys together produce the team's final employer feedback score. The end-of-project survey carries more of the weight, since it covers a longer arc.

The survey is administered shortly after your Final Presentation, while the full experience is still fresh.

---

## What you might see back

How much of the survey gets shared with your team is an institutional choice that varies by partner university. Most often, the three dimension scores and the "would you work with this team again" response come back to you. The open-ended instructor comment and the private professionalism rating stay with your instructor.

If the mid-project survey results came back to your team earlier in the course, the end-of-project version closes that loop. The dimensions are the same, and the comparison is what surfaces. A team that scored a 2 on Responsiveness at mid-project and a 4 at end-of-project has documented that the formative feedback led to real change. That comparison is one of the more substantive professional artifacts the course produces for your team.

---

## How this fits with the peer evaluation

The peer evaluation and the employer evaluation are designed to give you complementary final views.

- The peer evaluation is the inside view: how your teammates experienced your individual contribution across the full project.
- The employer evaluation is the outside view: how the team appeared to a working partner across the full project.

Both are graded items inside the Professional Engagement portion of your final grade. Because the employer cannot reliably assess individual contribution, all team members receive the same base employer score. If the peer evaluation reveals significant differences in how members contributed, your instructor may adjust individual employer-feedback scores within a defined range. That adjustment is a judgment call by your instructor, not an automatic calculation.

---

## What you can do with this

By Week 15, the team's professional habits have already formed. The end-of-project employer evaluation is the formal moment those habits are evaluated. If your team kept the commitments from the mid-project feedback debrief earlier in the course, you should expect that follow-through to show up in the ratings.

Beyond this course, "would you work with this team again" is the question that runs in the background of every working relationship you build. The course is the first place most learners see that question on paper. It is not the last.
