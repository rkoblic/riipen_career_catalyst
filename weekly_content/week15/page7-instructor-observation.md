# GRADED: Instructor Observation

Instructor Observation is the one graded item in Professional Engagement that doesn't come from a peer evaluation or an employer survey. It is a single holistic rating your instructor assigns at the end of the semester, based on patterns visible across the full 15 weeks. There is nothing to submit. This page explains what it is, what feeds it, and what role it plays.

This is one of five graded items that together make up Professional Engagement — see [**How Professional Engagement Is Assessed**](https://riipen.instructure.com/courses/107/pages/graded-professional-engagement) for the full picture and how the pieces fit together.

---

## What it is

A 1–4 holistic score that captures what your instructor has observed about your professional behavior across the course. Unlike the peer evaluation and employer feedback items, which use structured rating instruments, Instructor Observation is informed by patterns the instructor sees from outside the team and outside the employer relationship.

It is assigned once, at the end of the semester, after the Final Presentation and the End-of-Project Peer Evaluation.

---

## What feeds it

Four kinds of evidence inform the rating:

- **Checkpoint patterns.** Were the team checkpoints (Kickoff Summary, Direction Check Summary, Work-in-Progress Summary, Draft Deliverable, Near-Final Package) submitted on time, with genuine team effort? Was your participation visible across the arc, or did it concentrate on a few touchpoints?
- **Communication quality.** Were your emails, questions, and messages to the instructor (and on the platform) clear, professional, and timely? Did you reach out proactively when something needed clarification, or only react when prompted?
- **Feedback responsiveness.** When the instructor gave formative feedback on a checkpoint or graded work, did the next iteration show evidence the feedback had been taken on? Or did the same issue recur?
- **Professional judgment.** How did the team handle conflict, missed expectations, or moments of pressure? Did the team communicate early when something was off-track, or did problems surface late?

No single one of these is decisive. The rating reflects the pattern across all four.

---

## Why it exists alongside peer and employer feedback

The instructor's role is intentionally different from the other two sources. Peers see the inside of the team. Employers see the outside. The instructor sees the seams: the moments when something starts to wobble, the responses to feedback, the patterns visible over time that neither peers nor the employer have a frame for.

A team that looks fine from the inside (peers all rate each other well) and fine from the outside (the employer is happy) can still show patterns the instructor flags — checkpoints submitted at the last minute, late-stage communication when something has been off-track for weeks, formative feedback that didn't change anything. Conversely, a team that struggled internally or with their employer may still show strong instructor-observed habits (proactive communication, responsiveness, professional handling of difficulty). Instructor Observation captures the seam that peer and employer instruments don't.

---

## What you can do about it

The same things that produce a strong rating on the peer evaluation and the employer feedback also produce a strong instructor observation rating:

- Submit team checkpoints on time, with substantive content.
- Communicate proactively when something is off, not after the deadline.
- When you receive formative feedback, the next iteration of your work should show you engaged with it.
- Handle pressure moments professionally.

There is no special preparation for this item. By the time the score is assigned, the patterns are already in. The most useful framing is that the same professional habits you build for your team and for your employer also produce the instructor's read of how you worked.
