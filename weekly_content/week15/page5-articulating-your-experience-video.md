# Video Script: From "I helped" to "I led"

Here is a sentence a lot of learners use to describe what they just finished doing. "I helped with a project for a credit union."

That sentence does almost no work. It does not say what you did. It does not say what role you played. It does not say what the work produced. An interviewer reading it has no way to tell if the project was a two-week effort or a semester of substantive professional work. They move on to the next candidate.

Here is the same project described a different way. "I led the market research workstream for a regional credit union, translating three months of customer interview data into a strategic recommendations deck that informed the client's membership strategy."

Same work. Same role. Same outcome. The second version reads as real professional experience, and the first does not.

Three things change between them. The first is specificity. Not "a project" but "a regional credit union." Not "research" but "three months of customer interview data." The second is ownership. Not "I helped" but "I led." Not "we put together" but "I translated." The third is outcome. Not "we did research" but "the work informed the client's membership strategy."

Those three moves apply to every description of professional work you will write for the rest of your career. Resume bullets, LinkedIn profiles, interview answers, graduate school applications, grant proposals, performance reviews. Specificity, ownership, outcome.

The work you did in this course is real. Describe it that way.
