# Employer Outreach Message

In professional work, your first communication with a client sets the tone for the entire relationship. This message will be your team's introduction. It tells your employer contact who you are, that you've done your homework, and that you're ready to get started. You'll send it through the Riipen platform, but the same principles of professional email apply: clear structure, purposeful tone, and attention to detail.

---

## What This Message Needs to Accomplish

Your outreach message has three jobs:

1. **Introduce your team.** Keep it brief. One or two sentences about who you are as a group. The employer doesn't need full bios; they need to know the names and faces they'll be working with.

2. **Demonstrate awareness of the project.** Show that you've read the project brief and have a working understanding of what's being asked. This doesn't mean restating the brief back to them. Reference the project naturally, the way you would if someone asked "So what are you working on?"

3. **Request the kickoff meeting.** Propose a 60-minute meeting, suggest a few time options if possible, and ask what platform they prefer (Zoom, Teams, etc.). Make it easy for them to say yes.

---

## Tone and Professional Context

This person is a professional contact, not a professor or a TA. Write the way you'd write to a new colleague at work: respectful, warm, and specific. Avoid being overly formal ("Dear Sir/Madam, I hope this finds you well") or too casual ("Hey! We're super excited to work with you!").

You covered the fundamentals of professional correspondence earlier in the course. This is your first time applying them with a real professional audience. The same principles apply: clear purpose, purposeful paragraphs, and a professional tone.

---

## An Example

Here's what a first outreach message might look like. It's not a template to copy. It's an example of how the three jobs above come together in a short, professional message.

> Hi Daniel,
>
> We're your Career Catalyst project team for the term: Priya, Marcus, Salem, and Jamie, all in our final year at [University Name]. We're excited to be matched with NorthBay Outdoors on the customer retention research project.
>
> We've read through the brief together and have a working sense of where we'd like to begin: understanding how you currently identify repeat customers and what a useful research output would look like for your team. We'll bring our questions to the kickoff.
>
> Could we set up a 60-minute kickoff meeting next week? A few times that work on our end:
>
> - Tuesday, May 12, 10am to 1pm ET
> - Wednesday, May 13, after 2pm ET
> - Friday, May 15, 11am to 3pm ET
>
> If none of these work, we're happy to find another time. Let us know what platform you'd prefer (Zoom, Teams, Google Meet) and we'll send a calendar invite once we land on a time.
>
> Looking forward to meeting you,
>
> Priya, Marcus, Salem, and Jamie

The message is short. It introduces the team in one sentence, references the project specifically without restating the brief, and makes the kickoff request easy to say yes to by offering concrete times and asking about platform.

---

## Before You Send

**Everyone reviews, everyone agrees.** All team members should read the final version before it goes out. Make sure the message represents the whole team, not just the person who drafted it. One person drafts, everyone reviews, and you send it when the team is aligned.

**After you send it.** Don't just send and wait. If you haven't heard back within two to three business days, send a brief, polite follow-up. Employer contacts are busy professionals, and a gentle nudge is expected and appropriate. You may also need to handle logistics like confirming the meeting platform, sharing calendar invites, or coordinating time zones.

**AI use.** AI is permitted here. It can be helpful to draft the message yourself first, then use AI to refine your tone, tighten your language, or catch anything you missed. That's how thoughtful professionals use AI for correspondence: they write, then polish. It keeps your voice in the message and ensures the content is accurate to your actual situation.
