# Week 9 — Strengthening Your Work
## Content Development Plan

---

## Content Architecture

Week 9 is a **Pattern D (multiple anchors)** week — the densest in the Development phase. Three anchors run in parallel: the Draft Deliverable checkpoint (team submission), Development Check-In #2 (employer meeting, questions-based), and Reflection #3 (graded individual). Module content in this week serves two distinct anchor clusters: 9.02–9.04 feed the Draft Deliverable directly, and 9.14 feeds the Reflection. The Team Processing Session (9.06) is project work rather than instructional content, but module 9.05 serves as its direct preparation. Development Check-In #2 follows the same before/during/after structure established in Weeks 3, 5, and 7 — learners are familiar with the pattern, so these pages are lighter. The week reduces to eight content pieces: Week Overview → Strengthening Your Draft → Peer Evaluation Conversation → Development Check-In #2 Before/During/After (three pages) → Reflection #3 → What's Next.

### 2. Strengthening Your Draft
**Type:** Assignment prompt with embedded guidance
**Absorbs:** 9.02, 9.03, 9.04, 9.07

The primary production anchor for the week. Three modules (multi-author integration, actionability test, scope decisions) feed directly into the Draft Deliverable and are consolidated here rather than treated as standalone pages. The internal review gate (9.07) is embedded as a practice step at the end of the page — it is the immediate application of the module content before submission.

**Sub-elements:**

**Making your sections talk to each other**
- Why team-produced documents read like separate papers stapled together; how to identify the seams (AI-generated text, ~300 words)
- Practical integration techniques: unifying voice, building transitions, checking logical flow across sections written by different people (AI-generated text, ~300 words)
- Curated link: resource on editing for coherence in multi-author documents (Curated link + framing, ~6–8 min)

**The actionability test**
- Why "what did we find?" is not the same as "what should the employer do about it?"; what makes a recommendation actionable (AI-generated text, ~250 words)
- AI prompt strategy: using AI to stress-test recommendations for actionability — "read these recommendations and tell me which ones are too vague for a client to act on" (AI-generated text, ~150 words)

**Scope decisions: what to keep, what to defer**
- How to recognize scope creep at the midpoint; the three options — include, defer to future recommendations, decline (AI-generated text, ~200 words)
- How to communicate a scope boundary professionally to the team and to the employer (AI-generated text, ~150 words)

**Internal review gate**
- What the internal review gate is: reading the assembled draft as a complete document before an external audience sees it; this is a professional quality practice, not optional (AI-generated text, ~150 words)
- How to run it: each person reads the full draft end-to-end (not just their own sections), marks where sections don't connect or arguments are weak, then the team triages which problems to fix before submission (AI-generated text, ~200 words)

**Time check:** 9.02 (15 min) + 9.03 (15 min) + 9.04 (10 min) + 9.07 (60 min) = 100 min total → ~1,500 words text (~7–8 min reading) + curated link (~6–8 min) + thinking time + ~60 min doing the internal review gate. Instruction content fills the module time (40 min); project work time (60 min) is the gate itself. ✓

### 3. Having the Peer Evaluation Conversation
**Type:** Standalone module
**Absorbs:** 9.05

Module 9.05 directly prepares learners for the Team Processing Session (9.06). The Team Processing Session itself is project work (no content needed), but without this module the session lacks a practical protocol. Kept as a standalone page because it serves a distinct purpose from the production modules on page 2 and needs to be read before the session begins.

**Sub-elements:**

**What's happening in the Team Processing Session**
- Brief framing: three feedback sources converging — anonymized peer eval qualitative feedback, aggregated BARS averages, employer mid-project survey results; the session's purpose is to produce specific behavioral agreements, not to relitigate the evaluations (AI-generated text, ~200 words)

**How to bring up feedback results with your team**
- Practical protocol for opening the conversation; how to move from "here's what I received" to "here's what I want to discuss" without defensiveness or vagueness (AI-generated text, ~250 words)

**How to respond if feedback surprises you**
- What to do when your results don't match your own sense of how you contributed; the difference between pushing back on a specific rating and dismissing the feedback entirely (AI-generated text, ~200 words)

**From feedback to behavioral agreements**
- What a behavioral agreement is: specific, named, owned; what it is not: vague commitments or reassurances; how to negotiate specific changes the team will actually hold each other to (AI-generated text, ~200 words)
- Weak vs. strong agreement examples — one pair showing a vague commitment vs. a specific behavioral change (Scenario card, ~60 words)

**The team processing agreement**
- What to produce by end of the session: a brief written agreement documenting specific behavioral changes, who owns each, and how the team will know if it's happening; appended to Draft Deliverable submission (AI-generated text, ~150 words)
- Lightweight template: What we agreed to change | Who owns it | How we'll know (Template)

**Time check:** 9.05 (10 min) → ~1,000 words text (~5 min reading) + scenario card + template (~5 min) ≈ 10 min. ✓

### 7. Reflection #3
**Type:** Reflection prompt
**Absorbs:** 9.14, 9.15

Third DEAL reflection — framework is no longer new, so module 9.14 (DEAL Reflection — Going Deeper) is embedded here as preparation scaffolding immediately before the assignment. Focus is on the Examine stage: moving beyond description to examining assumptions, patterns, and professional implications. Draws from the richest period of the course (Weeks 7–9).

**Sub-elements:**

**Going deeper in your reflection**
- Why the Examine stage is where most reflections stall; what "examining" actually requires beyond summarizing what happened (AI-generated text, ~250 words)
- Techniques for deepening the Examine stage: name the assumption behind the action, identify the pattern across the experience, name the professional implication (AI-generated text, ~300 words)
- Strong vs. weak Examine examples: one pair showing surface description vs. genuine examination (Scenario card, ~80 words)

**Reflection #3 assignment**
- Focus area: a specific moment from Weeks 7–9 involving feedback or collaboration; use the DEAL model with higher depth expectation in the Examine stage (AI-generated text, ~200 words)
- AI guidance: AI-as-Socratic-partner prompt encouraged (e.g., "push back on this interpretation of what happened and tell me what else I might be missing") (AI-generated text, ~150 words)
- AI disclosure required; AI cannot supply the experience, the examination, or the articulated learning (AI-generated text, ~100 words)
- Submission instructions: `[PLATFORM: TBD]`

**Time check:** 9.14 (20 min) + 9.15 (90 min) = 110 min → ~1,080 words text (~5 min reading) + scenario card (~2 min) + thinking time + ~90 min drafting and writing. ✓
