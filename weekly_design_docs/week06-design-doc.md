## Weekly Activity Breakdown

Week 6 has two beats. The first closes the Discovery phase with Reflection #2 — students have completed Weeks 1–5 (kickoff, research, Context Analysis & Project Plan, Direction Check) and the reflection is sequenced at the start of the week so they can extract what's worth carrying forward before turning the page. The second beat starts production: a setup module on coordinating team-authored documents, the formatting standards that should be applied to the template before content goes in, three just-in-time instructional modules addressing the conditions students will hit during their first drafting sprint (working without certainty, moving from notes to draft, handling disagreement), then independent research and drafting. DEAL was introduced in Week 1 and students completed Reflection #1 in Week 3, so Reflection #2 here is a second application of a familiar framework — placing it first lets students treat it as the closing act of Discovery rather than as one more demand at the end of an already heavy production week. The production block is sized generously because Week 7 will require significant time on Check-In #1 preparation, leaving less time for independent drafting; students should take full advantage of the production space this week while it is available.

| Competency Area | Subskill | How This Week Builds It |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Critical Thinking** | Problem Framing | Module 6.05 addresses a specific manifestation of problem framing that is live for students this week: the inability to clearly frame a problem is often the reason production stalls. The module teaches students to distinguish types of ambiguity (missing information vs. unclear expectations vs. conflicting priorities) — a framing skill that applies directly to their production work in 6.08 and 6.09. |
| **Critical Thinking** | Research and Analysis | Activities 6.08 and 6.09 represent the first time students are producing independent research and drafting aimed at the final deliverable. Module 6.06 addresses the intermediate cognitive step that is most likely to cause students to stall: moving from a set of research notes to an organized outline, and from an outline to prose. The module explicitly teaches this as a separable process — synthesis first, production second — rather than treating drafting as a single undifferentiated task. |
| **Critical Thinking** | Prioritization | Module 6.05 teaches a concrete framework for prioritization under uncertainty: when do you have enough to proceed vs. when must you stop and gather more? | **Teamwork** | Collaborative Contribution | Module 6.03 (Starting Production as a Team) addresses the coordination decisions a team has to make at the start of any multi-author production effort: one shared document, section ownership, formatting standards, terminology. ## Deliverable Connection

**Reflection #2 → Career & Self-Development (Reflection, Self-Awareness)**

Graded; rubric-based. Individual submission. Contributes ~5% of the final grade as part of the Reflection Portfolio (25% total, five reflections). Students draw from the Discovery phase (Weeks 1–5) — the kickoff meeting, the research process, the Context Analysis & Project Plan, the Direction Check, team dynamics under pressure, or any moment of ambiguity or learning that emerged across the phase — and examine it using the DEAL model across three stages: Describe (what happened, factually and specifically), Examine (what assumptions were operating, what patterns emerge, what the experience reveals), and Articulate Learning (what transferable insight can be extracted that connects to professional practice beyond this project).

This is the second DEAL reflection in the course; the first was in Week 3 following the kickoff. The framework is familiar — the expectation here is not that students demonstrate they can use DEAL, but that they use it with more depth and honesty than they did in Week 3. The rubric dimensions are Accurate Description, Critical Examination, Honest Self-Assessment, and Articulation of Learning. Growth across the five reflections in the portfolio is itself evidence of learning.

The reflection is sequenced at the start of Week 6 so it functions as the closing act of Discovery. The Direction Check is the most recent significant Discovery experience and is fresh in students' minds; placing the reflection first lets them process the phase that just ended before they shift gears into production, rather than asking them to write a deep reflection at the end of an already heavy production week.

AI disclosure is required on submission via the Riipen platform. The core boundary is that AI cannot supply the experience, the examination, or the articulated learning — a reflection that could apply to any student in any project has not met the standard regardless of what tools were used to produce it.

## AI in This Week

**Permitted and encouraged:** Using AI as a Socratic thinking partner during the reflection writing process is encouraged — the prompt strategy for this was introduced in Week 1 alongside the DEAL framework and applied in Week 3. Students may use AI to deepen their examination of an experience by asking it to identify assumptions they haven't named, push back on surface-level observations, or ask follow-up questions that help surface genuine learning. AI can also support the notes-to-outline-to-draft process introduced in Module 6.06: using AI to help cluster and organize raw research notes, stress-test an outline structure before committing to drafting, or identify gaps in a section plan before prose production begins. Using AI to fill knowledge gaps that surface during drafting and to check draft sections for logical coherence and clarity are also permitted.

**Off-limits this week:** The reflection (6.02) must reflect the student's own experience and genuine learning from the Discovery phase. AI cannot supply the experience being described, generate the examination, or produce the articulated learning statement. A reflection that reads as though it could apply to any student in any project — lacking the specific details of what actually happened and what the student actually felt or thought — has not met the standard regardless of whether AI was used. On the production side, AI-generated content that students cannot verify or defend should not be in deliverable drafts: Check-In #1 in Week 7 is a live accountability mechanism.

**Disclosure required:** Yes — on Reflection #2 submission. If AI was not used, a one-sentence statement to that effect is sufficient.

**Prompt strategy introduced — From Research to Draft:** Module 6.06 introduces AI as a tool for moving from notes to outline to prose. Students use AI to organize and cluster raw notes rather than generate content, keeping the intellectual work their own while using AI to reduce the structural overhead of synthesis.

- *"Here are my raw research notes on [topic]: [notes]. Help me identify the three to five main themes or clusters across these notes. Don't summarize — just group them and label each cluster."*
- *"Here is my outline for my assigned section: [outline]. What gaps do you see? What questions would a reader have after reading this section that the outline doesn't seem to answer?"*
- *"I'm about to start drafting from this outline. Is there anything in the structure that seems out of order or that would be confusing to a reader?"*
