# Curriculum Production Update — Week of April 27

The past week was a sustained production sweep across the curriculum, organized around three threads: structural standardization, week-by-week voice and clarity refinement, and addressing some specific feedback I'd been carrying that finally got the time to land properly.

## Structural standardization

- Built two new internal guides defining canonical page structure: one for any page that explains a graded deliverable, one for the "what's next" pages at the end of each week.
- Applied both retroactively across weeks 2 through 15. Heading consistency, audience-and-use statements, and what's-next sections tightened across the board.
- Split standalone pages out of multi-purpose host pages so every required deliverable gets its own findable home: Team Rhythm (W2), Kickoff Summary and Standing Employer Meeting (W3), Context Analysis & Project Plan (W4), Direction Check Summary (W5).
- Reordered Week 3 so teams read about establishing the standing meeting *before* they propose the cadence at the kickoff itself.

## Voice and clarity across every produced week (1–15)

A focused pass through each week with the goal of making the writing sound less stilted and more like a thoughtful instructor talking to students. Recurring patterns cleaned up: overuse of em-dashes for emphasis, parallel three-part constructions ("First X, then Y, finally Z"), filler adverbs ("genuinely," "actually," "specifically"), closing lines that try too hard to wrap things up cleanly, and generic openers that don't ground in the student's specific moment.

Each week also got the substance fixes it specifically needed. A few worth calling out:

- **Week 4**: Split AI guidance into its own standalone page so it stops competing with the Context Analysis content.
- **Week 5**: Softened employer-read framing on the deliverable submission page; added concrete examples to the strategy page.
- **Weeks 14 + 15**: Removed two passages that equated engagement with body language (eye contact, posture, the Amy Cuddy power-pose). Rewrote to describe the underlying signal rather than one body's way of producing it.
- **Week 15**: Replaced hardcoded course-length references ("fifteen weeks," "the first thirteen") with project-relative phrasing so the content survives a change in course length.
- **Week 3**: Restructured three video scripts per the instructional video guide; page 7 reorganized around weak/strong contrast examples.

## Week 6 deepening

The "Making progress under uncertainty" page (your earlier flag) got tripled down on with three new sections — Choosing between paths, When to bring it to the employer, and Justifying and presenting your call — plus a worked example using a community-outreach scenario.

A second pass on Week 6 today went deeper: tightened the Reflection #2 setup, added "skim it before you need it" framing to two pages students might otherwise dismiss until they hit a hard moment, reordered the preparing-your-piece page so downstream owners learn early what to do this week, and added a Change Log template placeholder to the rubric-revisit page.

## Course-wide rename

Renamed the development-phase employer meetings from "Check-In #1 / #2" to "Development Check-In #1 / #2" across all learner-facing content, design docs, development plans, and guides. The bare "Check-In #1" was confusing — by Week 6, students have already had several meetings that could feel like check-ins (Kickoff, Direction Check), so calling the next one "Check-In #1" implied "first one ever." Adding the phase qualifier disambiguates. Touched 38 files in one sweep.
